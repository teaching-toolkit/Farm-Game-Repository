# Book file (canon)

The canon of the story. Read it before writing or changing anything; append to it after every session. (Tidied once, in the critical
pass of 7 Oct 2026; nothing was dropped.) Labels: ✅ decided · 💡 proposed · ❓ open · 🔒 hidden from the player.

## Dials 💡

- **Language:** a six-year-old can follow every sentence. Short words, short sentences. A small budget of stretch words (below).
- **Story:** built for an eight-year-old (the player): real wants, real consequences, a character who changes, a mystery she cannot
  see coming.
- **Address:** ✅ second person ("you"); people use the player's chosen name; no gendered word for the farmer anywhere, in English or
  German; nobody speaks *about* the farmer in the third person where the player hears it.
- **Texture:** dialogue-forward with **people** (villagers, Lark, the grandchildren in the frame). ✅ The Helpers do not talk, so their
  scenes are **physical comedy** plus what you take their sounds and looks to mean. Narration-forward only for wonder (the forest, a
  Helper's return, the night garden).
- **Form:** story boxes, not a book. 6 chapters, about 10–12 boxes each (2–4 sentences, 35–45 words), plus 7 frame scenes (120–200
  words). Roughly 9–10 thousand words in all (3.3 thousand built, 6.2 thousand added 14 Oct 2026). ✅ form (parent), 💡 numbers.
- **Time:** ✅ years and ages are never counted in the text (D11).

## Book sentence 💡

A girl who keeps busy so that nobody will ask what she did comes alone to the ashes of her family's farm to make one seed grow, but
the farm can only live again if she stops doing it alone and lets six lost helpers, and a village, back in.

## Fixed facts ✅

- Fenna, about 15, is the farmer (the player renames her); she is Minena's grandmother. Minena is the child in the frame.
- The frame appears once at the start and between chapters; inside a chapter there is only the story.
- The bulk of the story is the present of the game, with a mystery uncovered slowly.
- The fire began in the hay barn while the girl played with Mumyo; the imp caused it. She saw him; nobody believed her; later she was
  never sure. After the fire she **never saw him again**. Part of the family suspected her (leaning to an older cousin).
- The family worked for a Lord who always wanted more, who owes someone above him. Children worked dawn to dusk. The land dried out
  through a chain reaction nobody saw coming.
- After the fire the family left Hazelwood and split: parents, siblings and the cousin to a town; Fenna and her grandmother to a hill
  cottage, where the grandmother raised her with the book.
- Seven years later the grandmother dies and leaves the box. Fenna goes to the ruins "for perspective", drawn by a melody, and finds
  one seed and one patch of workable ground.
- She knows the village only vaguely and reconnects with it through the story, partly through her dishes.
- The melody comes from the conch (🔒🔒 the listener's own wholeness, projected; never in text).
- Six chapters, six pets; the medicine chapter between the old 4 and 5, with the badger. Pets give real jobs (automation).
- The six-line farm rhyme; the steward arrives in chapter 4; clues tied to ruins.
- The shell in the Moonstone Forest holds the magical ones; the squirrel may cross, the magpie may not. Lark is immortal and Wit-like.
- The forest is a separate screen in the medicine chapter.
- The farm lives on: one of Minena's uncles or aunts runs it in Minena's time. Minena visits often and loves it.
- Fenna's last words in the frame: *"If you meet a little fellow with bells, tell him I was never angry."*
- The bend (provisional): small bends earlier, then in chapter 6 she says aloud what she saw.

- (8 Oct 2026) Second person and a gender-open farmer; the farmer's character stays at the present level of detail. The seed: a ripe
  wheat stalk with one grain left, the rest nibbled off. Family names stay the same across generations. No religion. The Helpers do
  not talk. Lord Wexley. The orphanage favour stays.
