## D. The two first decisions

### D-B2 What Fenna remembers. **Settled by canon; no memo needed.**

Canon (bible §3; `liv-tilda-berit.md`; `telling.md` §4–5; timeline Y+1 to Y+7):

- She **remembers** the barn night: playing with Mumyo in the hay barn, his lights, the lantern on its hook outside and then inside, the empty bucket, running out, shouting about the lights, her last words to him, telling the grown-ups it was him.
- She **does not know whose hands** carried the lantern in. The book never answers this.
- Nobody believed her, so she stopped saying it. In time she was **not sure he was real** ("I looked the other way so I wouldn't find you").
- She **avoids** all of it. That is her spine: busy, so that nobody asks.

Applied: changes #22–#24 (`c2_barn`, `c2_hook`). What was left fits. The "So the bell-ringer was real" of the retelling is not in the material, and "She was one of those children" (5.26) reads as knowledge newly put into words, not as amnesia. **Kept and watched:** 3.24 "Fenna does not remember drawing it" (seed wording). A child of seven forgetting one drawing is plausible, and it fits her doubt about him. If the person wants zero risk of an "amnesia" reading, a labelled alternative is: *"Fenna looks at it for exactly as long as it takes to stop."* (drop the sentence about remembering).

### D-B3 What the village knows and believes about the fire. **Settled by canon except one part; memo for that part.**

Settled by canon:

- **Official record:** the Reeve's report, made the day after the fire, names a girl of eight seen running from the barn and a witness of twelve (bible §3: Liv told the Reeve; E-5.B15). It was never made public. Quill corrects it at the Fair.
- **What the village says:** "lightning" (bible §5, Q5 and Q18; the skipping rhyme; the Ballad).
- **What the village holds against the family:** the Mark, a fixed grievance about the share the burned farm stopped paying (bible §4), not a charge of arson. Tom's fear of the farm is a child's fear of a burned place.
- **What Liv kept:** the lights. At the Fair, "You were in the barn that night" is news to the village, not to Fenna. That is why it lands in public.

Applied: change #27 restores the kept seed's line "My mum says it was lightning." "Did she?" (E-2.B05 #2), so the reader hears the village's word plainly before the Fair.
