## 1. The village in one page

Hazelwood is a poor, tidy, nosy village of 31 households in a low wall, half an hour down the road from Moonedge, between a lazy stream (the Hazel) and a slow brown river (the Alder). A Lord lives on the hill, a mill sits on the stream, a landing sits on the river, and a bell hangs in the Hall (no chapel ✅). The valley has not had a proper farm since the fire, and the Lord needs coin to send downriver, so **since the fire every loaf in Hazelwood gets a chalk mark for the farm that burnt, and nobody likes it** (the Moonedge Mark). The village remembers the family: **one old woman and one old man have kept the place** (Maud leaves a bucket on the gatepost; Jan answers "Here!" for Moonedge), the rest are *polite and watching*. They are short of everything and good at being poor together: a pot on the Square where everybody puts one thing in, a stool outside every door for the man who comes to take, a cup for anybody who says a true thing on the longest night.

The farmer's whole way of speaking is a bowl. The village's whole way of listening is a bowl, too ("No bowl goes home empty"). That is the match the slice is built on, and the thaw is the story: the village is **won in steps**, not welcomed at the start.

## 3. The load-bearing ideas (revised)

1. **The village came.** On the night of the fire the whole village made a bucket chain from the stream at the foot of the rise up to the barn, about a hundred paces. It was too late. **That is in the village's memory; it is not in the village's gifts.** Only Maud keeps it: she leaves a bucket with a tin leaf under its rim on the Moonedge gatepost every late summer. The whole-village **Bucket Night** forms in chapter 3 (E-3.L11) and reverses in chapter 6 (E-6.L09). Never counted. *(Atlas, almanac 4.1.)*
2. **The Moonedge Mark** is a **fixed grievance**. "Since the fire, every loaf in Hazelwood gets a chalk mark for the farm that burnt. Nobody likes it." It does not shrink with what the farm pays. It ends in chapter 6, as a village act. Its use: it is why the village is cold to the family (rung 1–4) and why it can warm (rung 6). *(Atlas §6, foodways §1; E-2.L02, E-2.L10, E-6.L04.)* Ticket 07 confirms it fits the Lord's accounts.
3. **The Open Bowl.** "No bowl goes home empty." A bowl handed over with two hands cannot be refused and goes back with something in it. It explains why every delivery opens a trader, and it turns around in chapter 5 when the village feeds the farmer. *(Customs 1; foodways §6; almanac bark B27; E-5.L02.)*
4. **The Cup** (a rite, optional). At Longnight anyone who wants says one true thing; nobody answers with a word; the nearest person passes a cup. The farmer avoids it by running the soup (E-2.L05), says her smallest true thing in chapter 4 ("My feet hurt", E-4.L09), and the shipped `c6_bend` ends "Somebody passes you a cup". The village is **not** built to receive the confession; it has only this habit, meeting a surprise. *(Almanac 4.5.)*
5. **"Here!"** On Landing Day (every second sixth-day) Mei calls letters by name and each person shouts "Here!". Old Jan has answered for Moonedge alone as long as anyone remembers. Plant in chapter 2, the farmer answers herself in chapter 4, and at the Fair's roll call the family answers with Jan. *(E-2.L01, E-4.L07, E-6.L10.)*
6. **The Lightning Ballad** is the village's story, begun by Maud's one kind word. It is planted early (a skipping rhyme, E-2.L11) and performed by the Players before the bend (E-6.L05). *(→ ticket 04.)*
7. **The tune travels by humming.** The farmer hums without noticing; Rosa, Hilde, Tom, Ansel pick it up; by the Fair the village sings it, as shipped `c6_supper` says. *(→ ticket 06.)*
8. **The Harvest Fair is called by full tables**, not by a date: the `done:d_fair_*` triggers work in any season, and the Cloth Table has been laid and left empty since the fire. *(Almanac 4.4; E-6.L01.)*

## 11. Self-check against live-wire-style

- **Want meets obstacle:** each seed has a person who wants something and a thing in the way. Where the obstacle is the farmer's spine the joke carries the point (E-1.L05, E-2.L05, E-3.L02, E-3.L05, E-4.L09). - **Registers vary:** §6. The warm seeds are fewer, so each earns its place.
- **Nothing explains itself:** the Mark is explained once, by a child, in one sentence; customs are shown as acts and sayings.
- **Warm is earned:** every W follows a G or T inside its seed.
- **Cruelty:** none; the cold faces are people with reasons; the Lord is not made a villain here.
- **Stakes persist:** the Mark stays until chapter 6; the double chalk costs the farmer coin; the Marketday corner is given up; the Pot's quarrel lasts a week.
- **Dials:** language for a six-year-old (short customs and sayings; tick, Reckoning, Weighmaster gone), story for an eight-year-old (a village that has to be won, and pays for it).
