## The stance ladder and who warms first

The ✅ stance: the village distrusts the whole family and is poor. The grievance is the **Moonedge Mark** (a chalk thin moon on every loaf, sack and crossing "for the farm that burnt", a fixed grievance, not a meter; ends in chapter 6 as a village act, before Quill's paper). One person warms per chapter, and each warming has a cost:

| 2 | 2 curious | a boy asks, Hilde ties a knot, a loaf is chalked | Greenstem, Hilde | **cold face (a):** Appleby chalks the first loaf "double"; she earns less; a child sings the chalk rhyme to her face |

**The miller who still blames, and what thaws him.** Hob Millward blames Moonedge for the silt that stopped his wheel. Chapter 2: glares from the bridge. Chapter 3: the stream runs when she digs the pond; his one nod is his whole apology (E-3.R08) and it costs him (the Hurst charges a mill that runs); the grandfather salutes the new sails (E-3.L07). Chapter 6: the windcap sails turn again; Ella is allowed.

## The village when Quill arrives, and at the Fair

**Chapter 4 (Quill):** split. The reading of the lease at the gate (E-4.R42) makes the village hear "a half"; the Pot goes cold; Pieter walks slower, not late; Thorne (the Lord's gardener, who kept Moonedge cuttings alive) is kind in secret; Juno holds the Pot together by not choosing. **The Fair (chapter 6):** the village sets the tables before the paper arrives, wipes the chalk first (a village act with a cost), makes room on the Landing steps, and Maud puts a bowl into the father's hands.

## Six conflicts a child can follow

1. Appleby against the chalk (hates it, does it; ends by wiping it). 2. The miller against the stream (one nod). 3. Maud and Appleby stop speaking over Quill (a week; the Pot is cold). 4. Pieter against his own lantern (what he writes and what he is paid). 5. Ella against not being allowed (the sails). 6. Juno against her own scroll (a goat hears it). Smaller: Wren against hemlock; Hilde against the wind; Tom against his one boot.
