# Fenna

Round 3, draft 1 (7 Oct 2026). Labels: ✅ decided · 💡 proposed · ❓ open · 🔒 hidden. The player renames her; "Fenna" is the default.

## The rule above everything else ✅ (parent, 8 Oct 2026)

**The player is the farmer, and players are boys and girls who make their own avatar.** So in the game:

- **The texts are in the second person.** Inside a chapter the story speaks to *you*: "You find a wheat stalk with one grain left."
- **People call you by the name the player chose** (`{player}`), never "Fenna", never "she" or "he".
- **The frame:** grandchildren (Minena among them) ask *you* for a story. No "Grandma" or "Grandpa", and no "Oma/Opa" in German: they
  just say *you* (du). The grown-up you is never described.
- **Nobody talks *about* you in the third person** where the player can hear it ("she works hard", "er ist fleißig"): people talk *to* you.
  That keeps German free of gendered forms too.
- **Looks are the avatar's.** No hair, clothes or body in any text.
- **For us, the architects,** the character stays **Fenna, female**, in these files. It is a working name.

### How much character the farmer has 💡 (the parent asked: "what do you think?")

The current level is right, with one adjustment in *where* it shows. The spine, belief, want and need below stay as the hidden engine:
they decide what happens *to* you and how the world reacts. But the texts should rarely tell the player what *you* feel or do on your own
account. Two reasons: a second-person "you feel ashamed" is the fastest way to lose a player who does not feel it, and an 8-year-old
identifies through what they *do*, not through what they are told they are.

So: **the spine shows through the world, not through "you".** The tortoise makes you wait; the badger makes you sit; the goat eats your
list; Lark says "you've done eleven jobs before breakfast"; a villager says "you never stop, do you?". The player is invited, never
told. And the bend in chapter 6 is best **enacted** (the player taps *Sit down* or *Tell*) rather than narrated.

## The spine and its ladder (live-wire-style, character pass)

| Rung | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What does she want? | To make the one seed grow, by herself, and have something to show for herself before the town takes her. 💡 |
| 2 | What would she be embarrassed to admit? | She wants someone to tell her it is all right to stop. And to play. 💡 (This is the Mirror Crack: she says "I'm not tired" asleep on her feet.) |
| 3 | What drive explains everything? | **Keep busy, keep useful, never stop.** 💡 It explains her breakfast and her bedtime. |
| 4 | What does she hold true that is not? | **If I'm useful every minute, nobody can be cross with me, and nobody will ask what I did.** 💡 |
| 5 | Where did it come from? (off the page) | A child hauling a bucket bigger than herself at dawn, because the Lord's share was due. And one night in the barn she was *not* working, and the farm burned. So stopping = disaster. 🔒 |

**Need (an action, never stated):** stop, sit down, and say out loud what happened in the barn. Let the Helpers, and the village, help.

## The joke engine 💡

- **Escalator:** a tiny thing needs doing; she does it, and the thing next to it, and the thing next to that, until she has rebuilt a wall
  while looking for a spoon.
- **Loop:** every offer of help becomes "I'll just do it first". Three times per chapter, never learning (until she does).
- **Mirror Crack:** "I'm not tired." (Asleep.) "I don't need help." (Holding six buckets.)
- **Her opposites** do the rest: the tortoise will not hurry, the goat eats the plan, the badger makes tea first, and somebody keeps
  turning her work into a game (Mumyo).
- **In second person** the engine runs mostly through others: the joke is on the world's reaction to how much you do, not on "you".

Inside a joke she has no perspective on herself (non-negotiable 3). Between chapters she changes.

## Her arc across six chapters 💡 (the outline in Round 5 will test it)

| Ch | Where she is | The small step (a bend is an action, never a statement) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Ashes | Alone, doing everything. The bunny comes home: someone to look after. | She shares her porridge with a bunny. Still eats standing up. |
| 2 Homestead | The cottage, the pond. The tortoise cannot be hurried. | She sits by the pond and **waits** for it to fill, because the tortoise won't leave. First stop. |
| 3 Smallholding | The goat; the harvest dance; a tiny bell at the mill. | She **lets the goat clear the thistles** (first help accepted ✅). At the dance she works the cider, until a prank makes her laugh out loud. |
| 4 Village Trade | The steward counts. The pony carries her first big order into the village, and she is *seen*. | She tells the steward **no**: the field will rest this year. Her first "no" to *more*. |
| 5 Medicine | The badger. Illness in the village. | She **asks** the badger for help ✅. Possible low point 💡: she works herself ill, and the badger makes her rest (tea first). |
| 6 Master Farm | The night garden. The Fair. Her family. | **The bend** ✅ (provisional): she stops, sits, and says aloud what happened in the barn. It costs her armour (busy = safe) and risks blame. |

**After:** the world answers differently because she acted differently (the cousin, the village, the bells). The last line can still be funny.

## Relationships

- **Berit** (grandmother, gone): the voice in the book's margins. Fenna argues with the notes.
- **Her parents and siblings** (in the town): love from a distance; 💡 her mother's letters arrive by barge, asking her home.
- **The cousin** ❓: the person she least wants to see, and most needs to.
- **Young Greenstem:** the first person who remembers her kindly. Asks too many questions.
- **Lark:** says the things she will not; she cannot be cross at a joke.
- **Mumyo:** she never sees him. She feels watched, and cannot tell if that is good.
- **The Helpers:** she remembers them as a little girl remembers big animals. Now she is the grown-up for them.

## Never in the texts

Her name, gender, looks or age; "she"/"he" about the player; what "you" feel, beyond the minimum; the word "guilt"; any explanation of her spine; her saying she has changed. Someone else may notice, once.

## Inventions in this file 💡

The behaviours; "Just one more thing" (now a line *others* can tease you with); the bucket image; the chapter steps (except the two the parent set); the letters; the possible low point in chapter 5.
