# Part 0: What shaped the design

1. **The Helpers do not talk** ✅. Text may give a sound, a nod, a look, and *what you take it to mean* (the shipped badger: "Tea first, he seems to say"). Never a quoted line from a Helper. At most one translation per scene.
2. **They are named by the player.** No `{pet}` placeholder exists (node-map §3), so every text says "the bunny", "the goat". Default names (Pip, Mossy, Bramble, Hazel, Silverpaw, Alfie) are for the architects.
3. **Chapters mean something odd for a Helper.** A Helper arrives at the *end* of its own chapter and lives in every later one. The bunny has five chapters of life, the tortoise four, the goat three, the pony two, the badger one, the alpaca none (free play only). So the early Helpers carry the running gags, the late ones carry **omens before arrival** and free-play barks after.
4. **Delivery order** (`systems/story.gd`): frame, beat, discovery, letter, moment, visit, scene, bark; `moment`, `visit`, `letter` are rationed to one per 3 steps. A seed that must land *between* two shipped beats has to be a `beat` or `discovery`, or a page added to a shipped piece (Part 8); otherwise it waits for free play. Pieces show once; only barks return (cooldown 12). Nothing shows during the quiz or Rest.
5. **The pets' real jobs** (`rules/54-pets.json`): bunny weeds / stones; tortoise water / stones; goat weeds / compost; pony logs / sticks; badger herbs / compost; alpaca guard (pests) / wool. Level 2 opens when the *next* pet is home, level 3 adds a WHEN card, level 4 an "otherwise" rule; after the alpaca every pet is level 4. Mismatches with the rhyme are data wishes (Part 9).
6. **The rulings I rely on:** story wins on C11 (tortoise needs the pond, alpaca needs the night garden, badger is Silverpaw), C13 (the goat's deed is the scrub: `tap:patch:scrub`, `north_field_clear`), C16 (the collapse before the badger; nothing here hangs on the page), C19 (the letter under the hat is Mei's). The chapter-2 Mumyo trace is **not** covered here (06). Mumyo's *name* never reaches the player before the ch 6 telling: in texts he is "the one with bells" / "something small".
7. **Slice 01 alignment:** tune A is Berit's kitchen song (the rhyme, with words), tune B is the melody (wordless, from the forest). The three objects (book, pot, dented bucket) and Berit's hat exist; I use the hat and the bucket.
8. **Seeds are candidates, not a script.** The engine serves one piece at a time and rations moments; Part 7 shows what it will actually serve in each chapter.

# Part 1: The through-line, **the warning nobody read**

## 1.1 The idea 💡

Every Helper's comic quirk is what it did, in its own funny way, to try to stop something. The family called it a quirk.

- **Three are plain warnings.** The bunny thumped at the plough for three days. The tortoise sits on one exact spot: it is where the spring rose. The alpaca hummed at the hay barn seven nights and was moved to the far paddock, where she hummed too.
- **Three are protests or distress calls.** The goat ate the surveyor's stakes, the Lord's agent's hat and the scrub they were grubbing out (she was telling them what to eat). The pony hid lameness under her best pose until the spring she could not. The badger sat on the children's bed in the fever winter and would not be moved off it.

So the jokes the player laughs at in the present are the things the family would not read in the past, and the game's quiet argument ("read them this time") is carried by comedy, not explanation. A child who has laughed at the thump for five chapters hears it differently at the supper, and nobody says why. (Design line only: "this time somebody read the warning" never appears in any text.)

## 1.2 What the child did 🔒 💡 (an input for ticket 04)

The grown-ups in the family did not read the Helpers. The child Fenna (3 to 8 across the six leavings) did, in the small ways a child can: an apple a day to the lame pony; the bunny kept in a basket by the bed; water to the dry pond's edge in the dented bucket; the goat hidden in the cellar; the children "just tired". Her buckets, **ruling:** one bucket, two reasons (the tortoise's pond edge and the field, because the share was due). The origin of her habit stays the share and the fire; the pond is the kindest of the two reasons, and the only one the village later calls a game.

I hand 04 the open pieces, not a conclusion: did she hear the alpaca's last hum, and what did she do? If 04 does not use them, this section changes nothing in the Helpers' own seeds. It reaches the player only in Berit's pages (1.3) and through behaviour; the farmer never narrates it.

# Part 2: Helper grammar and how it sounds on the page

## 2.1 Rules shared by all six 💡

1. **A Helper has one signature sound, one yes, one no, one maybe, one tell, one appetite, one place it stands, one prop.** The child learns them across the game the way she learns a friend's face. By chapter 4 a six-year-old can read the farm's whole yard from the sounds alone.
2. **The text translates at most once per scene** ("... he seems to say"), and only after the sound word and the action have done the work.
3. **They never speak to each other either.** They talk by position: who stands behind whom, who waits, who lies down first.
4. **They are older than they look and never baby-cute.** They behave like staff with opinions on a small, short-handed estate.
5. **No Helper is ever cruel, and none is ever the butt of the joke.** The joke is the gap between a perfectly sensible creature and a world that does not read it.
6. **Mumyo is not looked at** (the farmer's habit, and the Helpers' etiquette). Nobody, human or animal, ever *looks straight at* the places where he is. The Helpers' behaviour around those places is how the player learns he is there.

## 3.1 🐰 The bunny (default Pip) · home at the end of engine chapter 1 · lives five chapters

**Spine ladder.**
- Spine: notice everything, panic politely.
- Belief (slightly wrong): *if I see it first, it cannot hurt us.* (Fenna's belief in another coat: *if I am useful every minute, nobody can be cross.* Both are on watch. Both cannot stop.)
- Want: to know where everyone is, always. Embarrassed want: to be told the fuss was not too much.
- Need (an action): hand the watch to someone else and sleep.
- The bend: the standing ear folds in a room with a guard in it. Cost: for the first time she is not looking.

**History and the warning (a plain warning).** Left when the clover was ploughed under for more wheat. She thumped at the plough for three days; the family said she was being silly. A child put her in a basket by the bed; she allowed it, and hated it, and did not leave. The night the plough finished, the thump went on in the empty field until dawn. In the morning only a hole was left. She never went far: she kept the one patch alive and nibbled the wheat stalk to its last grain, which she then did not eat.

**What she knows 🔒.** She saw the lights in the barn from the field edge. Open flame near dry things makes her thump the **roll**: long, unbroken, and nothing stops it but moving the flame. **The player hears the full roll for the first time at the supper (E-6.H08).** At the first fire (E-1.H01) it is only a *half-roll*: three thumps and a short roll that she gives up when you bank the fire.

- *Mumyo:* the one Helper who stayed. One soft thump at the bells ("I know you are there"), never the roll.
- *Fenna's spine:* mirror (both stand to eat; both on watch). She trusts Fenna *because* Fenna is busy.
- *Village:* children say she is a ghost (02 has the Ghost Patrol: check); adults say "what a nervous rabbit".

## 4.2 How the lines come back

✅ Memory, not magic: each line comes back to the farmer when its Helper comes home (shipped). Two additions 💡:

1. **The village keeps the rhyme too, and Maud is how** (ruling: no spell-like effect). Hazelwood's children skip to a song with the lost lines replaced by *la*.
