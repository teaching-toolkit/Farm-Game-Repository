Fresh-eye reviewer, 23 Oct 2026. ## Verdict (5 lines)

1. The core is the best idea in the run so far: each Helper's comic quirk was its last warning, the family called it a quirk, and the book argues "read them this time" only through jokes. Six distinct comic verbs, a real sound grammar, omen ladders before every return, and the lantern at the supper are worth keeping whole.
2. It is over-built at the edges (65 seeds; about 13 are filler, duplicates or other slices' work) and over-explained in exactly one place that matters: Berit's six pages say the idea out loud, in the third person ("the child"), which also bends the second-person rule.
3. The charge strips in Part 7 are idealised, not engine-ordered. In engine order, chapter 5 is overloaded (10 seeds in the 61-step chapter, 7 of them pony gags, and a four-T run), the tea scenes after the badger fire as one gag three times in a row, the goat's bend-1 warmth is lost under four giggles, and the post-bend warm run gets a fourth warm piece.
4. Contradictions are small and fixable: a bucket with three origins across the run, the lantern at the supper against slice 02's "hook outside, hay inside", the village rhyme against "memory, not magic", "seven years" in two seeds (D11), a feather on an alpaca. Nothing contradicts a locked fact; two items touch live canon and are already in QUESTIONS.
5. Recommendation: do not rewrite; run a short follow-up ticket "03b" (cuts, merges, re-keyed triggers, pages rewritten, ch5 and ch6 budgets), hand the Quill, Lord, fire and Lark material to 04 to 07, and have the manager set per-chapter seed budgets before 08a/08b merge anything.

## What holds (excellent; must not be lost)

- **The warning nobody read (1.1) as a comic design, not a message.** Bunny thumps, tortoise sits on the spring, alpaca hums: three are true warnings; goat, pony and badger are protests or masked distress calls (see fix 10 for the wording). Either way the player laughs for five chapters and hears the same sound differently at the supper. This is the "one object used twice" of live-wire done at book scale.
- **Six comic verbs that do not swap:** panic (bunny), endure (tortoise), consume (goat), display (pony), administer (badger), judge (alpaca). Each can be read from sound and posture alone.
- **The body-language grammar (Part 2):** signature sound, yes/no/maybe, one honest "tell" (standing ear, eyelids, chewing speed, tail, spectacles, ears), appetite, place, prop, fixed sound spellings. This is what lets a six-year-old read the yard by chapter 4, and it makes "they do not talk" a strength. Keep tables 2.2 to 2.4 almost as they are.
- **The omen ladder before every return** (prints and hutch; shell house and the trail; the list nibbled, then gone; hoofprints in pairs; swept in circles; the spit on the ledger and the one long note). It answers "who comes next?" without a word of plot.
- **The WHEN number as temperament (Part 6).** The child who sets "weeds >= N" is writing a program and choosing a character at once; nobody has to say so. Also the card idioms (clover leaf and pebble, moss card, bitten card, ribboned card, claw-scratch, wool tuft): a running snag whose idiom changes each time.
- **Dish and comfort item as apology (1.4)**: it turns six fetch quests into six answers to the warning. Free, deep, never stated.
- **Rhyme in return order, not leaving order**, with a Bargain clause per line (4.1): the ecology chain finally gets assembled for Q7 without a lecture. The village-keeps-the-rhyme idea (E-1.H76) is lovely once it is grounded (fix 11).
- **Individual seeds that are already finished:** E-6.H08 (lantern at the supper: the best seed in the slice), E-2.H11 (the seat in the dry pond), E-2.H12 (the bucket Loop), E-4.H15 (the reeds standoff), E-5.H45 (tea first in an emergency), E-5.H49 (the thimble), E-3.H65 with E-6.H55 and E-6.H19 (the Queue, the gap, the line closing), E-6.H72 (over the shell), E-4.H28 (the one bramble; the shears), E-5.H39 (the tail), E-4.H66 (the ladder: the pony's first "no" on something that does not matter), E-6.H09 (the ear that lies down).
- **Honesty of the author:** the ch5 T run, the supper-song conflict, the fire capstone and the Berit characterisation were all flagged by the author. This review mostly confirms and ranks.

### 3. "The warning nobody read": does it reveal itself through play?

Mostly yes in the Helpers' behaviour. It fails in four places:

- **Berit's six pages state the idea.** Four of six end on a guilt clause ("I did not ask why", "I thought it was a game", "I did not look"), and each page names "the child". Read in a row they are a lecture whose point is the one the gags were supposed to deliver silently. 1.3 even says the recurring clause is "I did not look".
- **"Look" now means two opposite things.** Berit's failure is that she did not look; Mumyo's etiquette (2.1 rule 6) is that nobody looks where he is. A child cannot hold both.
- **Third person.** "The child kept her in a basket" is a page talking about the player in the third person, which the locked second-person rule forbids. Slice 01's box note already shows the compliant form: Berit writes "you" ("If you are reading this, you went.").
- **Design lines that must stay design lines:** E-6.H08 "This time somebody read the warning", E-5.H07 "the player has just done to the bunny what the family did". Fine in a dossier; the 08c text pass must never say them.

Also, the warning idea is uneven: the bunny, the tortoise and the alpaca really warn; the goat (ate the surveyor's stakes), the pony (hid lameness under a pose) and the badger (would not move off the children's bed) protest or signal distress. Say "each tried, in its own funny way, to stop something" and keep the fit loose.

One more thing the idea cannot stand: **the child who noticed (1.2) exonerates Fenna too well.** If she read six warnings and carried the apple and the bucket, how does she not read the seventh (the alpaca humming at the hay barn for seven nights, and "she heard the hum")? That is ticket 04's gift, not a defect: the guilt could be "I heard the hum and went in anyway" (or the hum was about her and the imp's nightly lantern). But if 04 does not use it, 1.2 makes the barn story weaker, not stronger.

### 4. Bottleneck (one notch)

Of the four listening questions the thinnest is **"does it land somewhere" (change)**. About a third of the "What changes" lines are real (a fact, an object, a person's stance, a clock: H07, H08, H11, H19, H22, H28, H45, H49, H55). The rest end on "the pair becomes a running gag", "the player has seen X", "the village learns to anticipate him". Related and worse for an eight-year-old: **costs do not persist.** Every bend has a stated cost in the sheets (the bunny stops looking, the pony gives up being best-liked) and almost none is paid on screen: Quill withdraws the timber request with "I had not known" (E-6.H41), the eaten letter costs nothing (E-6.H30), the goat's hat is rescued, the spit lands on a book and a hat only. One notch up: make about ten "What changes" lines leave a thing that stays on the farm (the one bramble is a tile that can never be planted; the stone path from the pond to the house; a hat with the dark mark; the thimble on the sill) and price at least two bends in something the child will miss.
