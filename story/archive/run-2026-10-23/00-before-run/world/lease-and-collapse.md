# The lease and the collapse

Round 2, draft 1 (7 Oct 2026). Labels: ✅ decided · 💡 proposed · ❓ open · 🔒 hidden. This is the ecology the game teaches. Each link of the
chain is a system the game already has, so **playing the game is the repair**.

## The Lord, the steward, the Count 💡

- **The Lord of Hazelwood:** ✅ **Lord Wexley**. He inherited a manor with debts. His spine is *More*, but he
  is afraid, not greedy: he owes a **Count downriver** (💡 who lends against the harvest), and each year the Count wants more. ✅
- **The steward, Master Quill** (💡 name): thin, careful, keeps the ledgers. Stiff but fair. He knows the arithmetic is wrong and
  has never said so. He comes to Moonedge in chapter 4 ✅ to see why the old farm smokes again, and is surprised by what he finds.
- **The Count:** never seen. He is only a name in the ledger, which is part of the point: nobody at the bottom can argue with him.

## Why the family said yes 💡

They were afraid of losing the farm. The lease allowed no argument: a good year raised the share, a bad year raised it too ("to catch up").
**Berit** said no and was overruled by her son, Fenna's father, who believed the next year would be easier. He is not a villain; he is tired.

## The chain reaction, and what the player repairs 🔒→ revealed bit by bit

| # | What went wrong | What it did | What the player finds | What the player does (game system) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No fallow, wheat after wheat | The soil gets thin and pale. Crops "tired" | Tired soil; the matriarch's note about rest | **Crop rotation** (tired soil −15 %; rested soil after beans or clover +10 %) |
| 2 | Straw sold, not composted | Soil loses what feeds it | A clean, dry, empty compost corner (`ruins_compost`) | **Compost and fertiliser** (compost bin, manure, ash) |
| 3 | The forest edge cut | The hillside loses shade and roots; frost heaves more stones; wind dries the field | Too many stumps for a small farm (`ruins_lumber`, `clear_stumps`) | **Stones and stumps**; **trees along the road; hedges**; orchard |
| 4 | The spring pond drained | The stream silts; the well drops; the mill wheel stops; the lower meadow turns to marsh | A dry silted pond; a stopped mill (`ruins_windmill`) | **Dig out the pond**; **wells**; **water carried by hand**, **channels**; **drain the marsh** (River Meadow) |
| 5 | Clover ploughed under, brambles grubbed | No green manure; no wild edges for bees, birds and bunnies; **pests have no enemies** | Bare fields; birds in the seed | **Clover; scarecrows; hedgerows; bees** |
| 6 | The Helpers worked as tools, then ignored | They leave, one by one | An empty harness, a dry pond, a clean pasture | **The pets return**, each tied to a chapter |
| 7 | Hay left dry | The fire | The scorch that began low, in the hay corner (`ruins_barn`) | **Barn tiers; fire safety; a well near the barn** |

Everything on the right is *already in the game*. The story's job is to say, now and then, **why**.

## What the Lord wanted, in the end 🔒

He got nothing. The fire took the barn, the stock and the family's labour. The farm that was meant to pay the Count never paid again. In
chapter 4 the steward finds the old lease and the numbers: *more* would have ended the farm in two more years even without the fire.
Whether the Lord comes to Moonedge himself (❓ D3): 💡 he stays offstage until chapter 6, or never; the Fair can end with a note from him.

## The theme, said simply (never in the text, always in the shape)

*More* destroys what *enough* keeps alive. Fenna's own spine (keep busy, never stop) is a small private version of the Lord's.
The farm's old rule is on the first page of the book: **Start small. Small is plenty.**
