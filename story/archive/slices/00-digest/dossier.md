# Part 1: Canon digest

## 1.1 The book

*Hazelwood Harvest* ✅ (title). A game, not a book: story boxes of 2 to 4 sentences (pages of 54 words or fewer), second person, the player's chosen name as `{player}`. Six chapters, six Helpers, one hidden truth, one frame. Book sentence 💡: *A girl who keeps busy so that nobody will ask what she did comes alone to the ashes of her family's farm to make one seed grow, but the farm can only live again if she stops doing it alone and lets six lost helpers, and a village, back in.* Dials 💡: language comfortable for a six-year-old, story built for an eight-year-old, texture dialogue-forward with people and narration-forward for wonder. The Helpers never talk; their scenes are physical comedy and what you take a look or sound to mean.

**The frame** ✅: a very old you in bed, grandchildren on the floor (Minena, 7, in front), "Tell us about the farm that burned down." Seven bedside scenes: before chapter 1 and after each chapter (the last carries the request *"If you meet a little fellow with bells, tell him I was never angry."*). The frame never interrupts a chapter. The grown-up farmer is never described. Minena's own story (*Minena 01*, the conch) happens about 55 years after the game starts.

## 1.2 The people

- **The farmer.** For the architects, **Fenna**, female, born Y−8, 8 at the fire, 15 at the start ✅, Minena's grandmother ✅. For the player: *you*, no gender word, no looks, never described, never spoken about in the third person. Spine 💡: keep busy, keep useful, never stop (wrong belief: if I am useful every minute nobody can be cross with me or ask what I did). Need, never stated: stop, sit, say aloud what happened in the barn, let others help. She hums the tune without noticing.
- **Berit** 💡, her father's mother, born Y−62, died Y+7 at 69, wrote the farm book (thin brown ink, dry margin notes), overruled by her son, raised Fenna on chores in a hill cottage, never wrote the rhyme down. In the game only a handwriting and a still-room.
- **The family** 💡: father (tired, decent, said yes to the Lord; carter in Weir), mother (practical, letters by barge), two younger siblings (5 and 2 at the fire, unnamed). **The cousin** ✅ a girl (daughter of the father's sister), 12 at the fire, about 19 now, unnamed; spine: somebody must be to blame; saw something that night.
- **Mumyo** ✅: hand-sized imp, white hair, grey vest of bells, lonely, the girl's only playmate; the fire began while they played. Present only as traces (bells, moved things, a night garden); Fenna never sees him again ✅; the player sometimes does. No voice.
- **Lark** ✅: practically immortal, Wit-like; says true things as jokes and never pushes; rust coat, odd buttons, sorts things absurdly, "Probably fine." No bells.
- **The Six Helpers** ✅ (see 1.9) and the **squirrel** (Nutmeg, named by Lark in free play; the golden acorns are his). The magpie never leaves the shell ✅.
- **The Lord** ✅ **Lord Wexley**, offstage, afraid not wicked, owes a **Count downriver** 💡. **Master Quill** 💡, steward, thin, a pencil, counts everything; arrives chapter 4 ✅; his numbers bring the share to a tenth. **Pieter** 💡, night watchman, lantern in daylight, collects the share, almost apologises; sees a tiny light over the marsh.
- **Hazelwood** 💡 (names mostly kept for continuity with *The Forest Within* ✅): young **Greenstem** (a boy of Fenna's age, asks too many questions, becomes Wren's pupil; Minena's Mr. Greenstem), **Wren** (healer from the next valley, a woman, rule: bring me the basket), **Granny Maud** (Berit's oldest friend, a runaway goat), **Ella** and the **miller** (he still blames Moonedge until the stream runs; one nod), Hilde the washerwoman, Old Jan (ex-farmhand: "that goat could count"), Otto the ferryman (took you across the night you left), Tom the shepherd boy, Captain Mei (barge, carries the letters), Bo and old Odinson (smithy), Mistress Appleby (bakery), the Solemate cobblers, Rosa the fiddler, Ansel the bell-ringer, Teacher Ines, Anna and Ben, Ferdinand the lamp-maker, Kit the poet, Lucia the painter, Matron Elsie, Elder Agnes, Mayor Juno, Young Sami, Vega the astronomer, the twins Lotte and Linus, Ginger the cat; and unnamed: the seed seller (she), the bookseller (he), the travelling merchant at the gate (he).

## 1.3 The places

**Moonedge** (the farm), on a gentle hillside at the forest edge, Hazel stream below; the first field is full of moonstones. **Hazelwood**, half an hour down the road: square with well and notice board, bakery, smithy, mill on the stream, village hall (no chapel ✅), manor on the hill above. **The Moonstone Forest** to the west and north, white stones that hum, the **conch** (a house-high white shell) deep inside, many hours' walk; the game never enters the shell ✅; chapter 5 adds a forest screen. **The River Meadow** marsh below the farm (the night garden). **Weir**, a river town days downriver (parents). The hill cottage. The next valley (Wren). The coast (flax). The map: forest from the top left, village road from the top right.

## 1.4 Time

Y0 is the fire, late summer, after four dry months. Slide 💡: share a tenth then a fifth (Y−14), a third (Y−10), a half (Y−5); no fallow, straw sold, forest edge cut, pond drained (Y−6), clover ploughed, brambles grubbed. The Helpers left in the order pony (Y−5), bunny (Y−4), tortoise (Y−3), goat (Y−2), badger (Y−1), alpaca (Y0, at dawn after the fire). After the fire: lease ended, family split ✅ (parents, siblings, cousin to Weir; Fenna and Berit to a hill cottage). Y+7 winter Berit dies and leaves a box (the book, a tin pot, a dented bucket); early spring a melody starts; Fenna asks for "a few weeks" 💡 and walks to the ruins. **The game starts.** Game time is never counted in player texts ✅ (D11). The clock is the Time Quiz: 30 steps a season, about 8 seasons in chapter 1 alone, 9 to 12 game years in a full playthrough.

## 1.5 Rules of the world

The Bargain 💡: the Helpers help, the people take only what the land can give back. Magic is small and sideways: a bell, a hint, a returned animal; it never does the farmer's work. Helpers look like clever animals and leave when the Bargain breaks. Mumyo's pranks are harmless (moved, never lost). Lark never solves. **The melody** ✅ comes from the conch; its deepest layer 🔒🔒 (the listener's own wholeness, projected) is never in any text. Belief is light and pagan ✅ (a bowl of milk, a word to the well), no religion, no chapel. Delivery 💡: kinds are frame, beat (critical path), discovery (you tapped it), moment, visit, scene, letter, bark (a bubble you tap away); one unprompted piece per three steps; never during the quiz or Rest; "dishes are the research" (deliveries open merchants and warm the village). Nothing punishes the child, with one parent-approved exception (poison from an unchecked plant in chapter 5).

## 3.3 Ranked gaps

Ranked by the charter's order (people who want something; a deadline that bites; mystery steps; giggles; immersion), then structure.

1. **Wants are thin.** Of the 134 staged non-bark pieces, 88 (66 %) are Reward, Notice, Arrival or Level-up; only 22 put an obstacle in front of a person. The shipped beats have wants (Pieter, Quill, Wren, the Lord's letters, the cousin) but almost all favour-givers are one-scene gift receivers who never ask again: a person's want does not persist across chapters. *Cost:* the village is a row of thankful faces, not neighbours with problems. *Take:* tickets 05, 02.
2. **No deadline bites.** The only clock with teeth is the Lord's share (engine, from chapter 3) and the mother's "before the frost", which the game's own seasons disprove (C1). Chapter 5 has a cough but no one named is sick and nothing worsens; the Fair has no date; chapters 1 and 2 have no due date at all. *Take:* ticket 07.
3. **The mystery has one clue step where it needs three.** Q6, Q7, Q9 and Q11 are missing a step (the drained pond, the Helpers' pages, the Count, the melody-naming); Q2, Q4, Q6 and Q12 sit on optional nodes and can be missed entirely; Q9's lease can come after Quill leaves; Q10's page is fragile. The final answer is told offstage, and Mumyo is never named. *Take:* ticket 04, 06.
4. **Giggles are thin where the story is heavy.** Shipped chapter 1 opens warm, warm, warm, awe; chapter 5 has no giggle for ten pieces and a run of three tense; chapter 6 ends warm, awe, warm. The staged layer is 53 % warm and 9 % tense and has long warm runs; it mostly fills silence, it does not escalate. *Take:* every ticket; reviewers.
5. **The six Helpers are events, not characters.** Each appears in 1 to 4 pieces in all (shipped plus staged). They never meet each other, never act on the farm after their return except by a job card, and their running gags do not exist yet. Their automation (the job board, levels 2 to 4) has no story at all. *Take:* ticket 03.
6. **The cousin has no name or presence until she accuses you.** The most important human in the ending is a plant in three letters. *Take:* ticket 04, 05.
