# 1. The scene in one picture

**One telling, in two rooms, to two kinds of listener, and finished in a third.**

| | **Half 1: the Fair** | **Half 2: the garden** | **The frame** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Listeners** | the people; Liv asks, the village hears | the dark; Mumyo (unseen); the alpaca (back turned); the player | Minena |
| **What can be said** | what a person can say to people: the barn, the play, the lantern, the bucket, the running, the lights | what can only be said to him: his name, the friendship, "go away", "I told them it was you", the doubt | "the truth, all of it" (offstage), the name said to a living person, the request |
| **The name Mumyo** | **not said** ("I'm not going to say who. Not here.") | **said once, first, alone on a page** | said to Minena |
| **What answers** | Liv, the Cup, a mother's sentence; **no bells at all** | a *tink*, then two soft rings | a promise |
| **What it costs** | the armour; the public; the disobeyed "Not a word"; saying "play" | the secret; the unsaid sentence | the last of the cost |
| **Engine** | automatic after `c6_crisis` (✅ C4) | automatic, after the supper and the lantern | automatic, after the garden |

This honours every ruling: the bend is automatic and enacted by what the player has seen and done; the player **is** told the content in the farmer's own words, in full, across the two halves; the name reaches the player only through the farmer's mouth; "told something nobody else knows" is the garden, where the player and the dark are the only listeners; the frame's closing line is paid.

**What each half must NOT say** (so nothing is told twice): Half 1 does not name him and does not say what she shouted at him. Half 2 does not retell the lantern, the hay or the running; it needs one line for the lantern and goes on.

# 4. Half 1: the Fair, as an exchange with Liv

## 4.1 Staging

After the Players' Ballad (E-6.B21) and Liv standing (E-6.B22, shipped `c6_crisis`). The farmer is at the Cloth Table with the bucket in her hand. The Helpers who live at the farm are in the square; the alpaca is not yet home. Mumyo is under the long table among the children's boots, his bells silent (a glimpse in the picture; the farmer does not look). Lark leans on a post at the back.

## 4.2 Page plan (sketch lines; every page ≤ 54 words ✅, mostly 20 to 35)

Page types alternate: action, one line, reaction. Liv asks; the farmer answers in one line each, and stops where she cannot go on.

| P | Type | Content | Sketch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **action** | You put the bucket down in the dust. It is the first time you have set it down in front of anyone. You sit on the nearest bench with nothing in your hands. Liv stands over you. | Liv: "Where were you?" You: "In the barn." |
| 2 | **one line** | "Doing what?" The shortest hard thing: she was *playing*. The bucket as a pass. | "Playing. I took the bucket in with me. Nobody asks a child with a bucket where she's going." |
| 3 | **reaction** | "Who with?" She will not say. The village's own rule (you may say as much as you can). Linus's whisper is the one joke. | "I'm not going to say. Not here." Linus, under his breath: "I *told* you." Lotte kicks him. |
| 4 | **one line** | "And the lantern?" The truth with the gap in it: it hung outside on its hook, then it was inside, and she does not know whose hands. | "It was on the hook, outside. Then it was in the hay. I think I carried it. Maybe it was the lights. I don't know." |
| 5 | **one line** | The hay; the bucket; the running; the lights; the silence. | "It went up as if it had been waiting. The bucket was empty. I hit it anyway. Then I ran. I shouted about the lights. Nobody believed me, so I stopped." |
| 6 | **reaction** | Liv sits. "I saw the lights too. I never told anyone." Then the bucket, two meanings in four lines. | Liv: "You had the bucket. I thought you were running away with it." You: "It was empty." |
| 7 | **reaction** | The Cup (Maud's is the first, 02 owns her line). Her father crosses the table. Her mother's one plain sentence. | Mother, no brackets: "You were a child." The farmer does not answer. |

*(Sketch P4 was "my arm hit it"; the manager's ruling replaces it with the gap. If the six-year-old child test finds "I think I carried it" too hard, use "it fell" in Half 1 and keep the uncertainty for the garden.)*

## 4.3 Who reacts (capped at four on the page; the rest in the picture)

| Who | What they do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Liv** | stands through P1 to P5, hands shaking, a stone in one fist; sits at P6, not touching, then closer | her bend: asking, then listening |
| **The father** | rises from the long table, walks its length, sits on the farmer's other side, hands on his knees (as on Cobble's neck) | his silence breaks without a sentence |
| **Linus** | "I *told* you" (P3); Lotte kicks him | **the deflating joke** in a tense stretch: it resets the dread and buys the next build |
| **Lark** | stands very still at the back and **makes no joke** | the player who knows him knows how bad it is |
| *in the picture only* | Quill's pencil in his pocket; Pieter's lantern low; Wren's arms unfolding; Greenstem next to Liv; Hilde's hand on her string; the younger sibling taking the farmer's hand; the bunny's ear not standing; Mumyo under the table; **no bell at all** | no page can carry fifteen reactions |

The mother's sentence is not a "reaction"; it is the plain adult line (P7).

## 4.4 What the Fair costs the farmer

1. **The armour.** She sits, in public, with nothing in her hands.
2. **The word "play".**
3. **"Not a word."** Her father said it; she is saying it in front of him.
4. **The risk of being disbelieved again.** Corroboration comes from the person she feared most.
5. **Mumyo's secret.** She holds back the name, and a true thing that stops short costs her the whole telling. (The village's custom: **true things**, slice 02.)

# 5. Half 2: the garden (the confession proper)

## 5.1 Why she is there (the critical-path reason) 💡

In story terms: **the alpaca will not come home until the small one in the dark has been counted.** She is the one who counts the herd (03). From the bend on she stands on the ridge above the marsh facing the reeds, humming one low note (E-6.B25, a bark on `tap:patch:marsh`); she comes down only after the meadow is turned and the night garden opens. This gives `pet_alpaca` a reason to require `river_meadow_drain` (the data wish, C11) and makes the garden the alpaca's homecoming condition. **Fallback if the data wish is refused:** Half 2 happens at **the barn doorframe** the farmer has walked round since chapter 1 (E-1.B02); she turns the bucket over and sits where she never went. That loses "the night garden" as the place of the telling (the ladder's 6.11 is ✅-adjacent); use it only then.

## 5.2 Staging

After the supper and the lantern. The night garden, white flowers open, a stone at the edge. The **alpaca** walks behind her and turns to face outward, her back to the garden (03 E-6.H58). The farmer **turns the bucket over and sits on it**, and does not look at the flowers. The bells are quiet. The player may see a hand-sized shape behind her shoulder (a glimpse; 06 owns the budget). *The thing she has carried since chapter 1 is what she sits on.*

## 5.3 Page plan (sketch lines; every page ≤ 54 words)

| P | Beat | Content | Sketch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arrival** | night; flowers; the alpaca's back; the bucket becomes a seat | (narration) |
| 2 | **The name** | **One word on a page.** The first time the name is said in the game. | "Mumyo." |
| 3 | **The friendship** | At the Fair she said she wasn't alone. It was him. He came when she was carrying water for the tortoise; he thought the bucket was funny; he rode on the handle; one bell was cracked and she never told him. (The only smile in the telling.) | "You laughed at the bucket. You rode on the handle." |
| 4 | **The lights; the lantern** | His lights; she laughed for the first time that summer. One line for the lantern: she does not know whose hands, and she would rather it was hers. | "You made lights. I laughed. The lantern came in. I don't know whose hands. I'd rather it was mine." |
| 5 | **The words** | He tried to blow it out; it got bigger. Then: "go away". **A single *tink*.** | "I said go away. I said I never wanted to see you again." |
| 6 | **The blame, the doubt** | She told them it was him. She looked the other way so as not to find him; she was not sure he was real. | "I told them it was you. That wasn't fair. I'm sorry. I looked the other way so I wouldn't find you." |
| 7 | **The sentence she cannot say** | She starts the kind sentence; it will not finish. **She does not ask him to come out.** A *tink*; then **two soft rings**. She does not know what they mean. The player does. | "I never ... I wasn't ..." |
| 8 | **Home** | She stands, takes the bucket, goes. The flowers close. The alpaca's ear turns, once. | (narration) |

About 110 words in her mouth. One named feeling is allowed in the chapter; "I'm sorry" is an act, not a feeling word.

*Why "I'd rather it was mine":* she gives him the sentence nobody gave her (not only yours), and the player hears her take the blame she is not sure is hers. The mother's "You were a child." has already been said at the Fair and was not answered; the garden is where the player hears it land.

## 5.4 Why she does not say "I was never angry" 💡

She was not *never* angry; she shouted. "I was never angry" is true as a judgement on the years and false as a report of one minute, and she is fifteen and honest and will not say a half-true thing to the one creature who heard the whole. What she wants to say is *you did not make me send you away*, and the only person he could hear that from is someone with no minute behind her. That is why the message goes through **Minena**: "tell him I was never angry" is true and simple from a child.

## 5.5 What she does not ask

She does not ask him to come out. Two spines lock: hers (never ask for what you want) and his (*if she saw me she would send me away*; she once did). The player sees both. The cost the book keeps (✅): she never sees him again; the sentence travels to Minena.

## 6.2 The answers, in order

1. **At the Fair: no bells at all.** The player who has lived with them for six chapters feels the hole.
2. **At the garden: a *tink* at the words** ("go away") **and, at the end, two soft rings.** *Sorry. I'm here.* Not "I forgive you"; not a face.
3. **After:** the night-flowers on the step and the empty milk bowl are **06's** (C8; E-6.M-series), not 04's.

# 8. How the frame's closing request pays off

**Frame 6, after the garden:** Minena whispers, "Tell me the imp was real." The old you tells her **the truth, all of it** (offstage; the player already has it). New in these two pages:

1. **The name is said to a living person for the first time.** "His name was Mumyo." (The garden said it to the dark; the Fair withheld it.)
2. **The promise of frame 0 is kept by reversal.** Frame 0: "I never start with the fire. I start with the ash." Frame 6: "*Tonight I'll start with the fire.*" Nobody comments.
3. **A funny last beat that confirms the change** (live-wire §13: end funny, do not restore): the old you tells it, and halfway through stops. "Are you tired?" "I am not." (You are asleep. Minena counts to ten.) You wake. "Where was I?" "The fire." "Ah." The Mirror Crack of chapter 1 ("I'm not tired", lying very straight) returns, and this time a child is allowed to wait.
4. **The request** (✅ unchanged, still the frame's last spoken line): *"If you meet a little fellow with bells, tell him I was never angry."* Minena nods very seriously; that night she dreams the melody.

**Picture note for frame 6:** the bucket at the bedside holds Minena's stones and feathers **and a few flat grey river stones that do not match**. No word. (Liv's stones; the farmer took them in. Counted nowhere.)

❓ **For the parent:** does Minena know Mumyo's name before she meets him in *The Forest Within* / *Minena 01*? This design makes the old you tell her.

# 10. Engine and form notes

- **Length:** Half 1, 7 pages; Half 2, 8 pages (several one line). ❓ the box must accept 7 and 8 pages; unskippable.
