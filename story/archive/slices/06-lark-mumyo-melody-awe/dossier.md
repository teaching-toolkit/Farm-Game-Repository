**The one idea that holds the slice together 💡.** Three things in this book are about *stopping*, and none of them may say so: the melody has a **rest** in it that the farmer always hurries over; Mumyo's every trace is an invitation to **look and stay**; Lark's every joke has the true thing in its **second clause**, where she can pass it by. The farmer's spine (never stop) is the obstacle to all three. So the slice is not three characters, it is one shape seen three ways: *a gap that someone else leaves for you*. The comedy is that she steps over every gap; the cost is that the gap stays; the payoff is that in chapter 6 she sits, and the village sings into the gap she has finally left. Nobody says it. # 1. Lark

## 1.1 The sheet (live-wire `character.md` ladder, only what the book needs) 💡

| Rung | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 What he wants | To see whether she finds it herself. |
| 2 What he would be embarrassed to admit | To be asked to stay. |

| 4 Slightly wrong belief | If I told her straight it would break her. (Partly true, so he holds it.) |
| 5 Origin 🔒 | He has seen people told the truth too early and wrecked by it. |

**Joke engine in one sentence:** the farmer is always doing the next thing; Lark is always doing something pointless beside her; the true thing is in the second half of a sentence whose first half is a gag, so a person who is busy hears the gag and a person who stops hears the rest.

**Clash with the farmer's spine:** she cannot stop, he cannot stay. Each is the other's embarrassed want. They never say so.

## 1.2 The anatomy of a Lark visit (the rule-set) 💡

1. **He arrives without a sound of arriving.** No bell, no footstep, no "Lark came". The first box that mentions him already has him on a fence, a crate or a stair.
2. **He sits lower than she stands**, or he stands doing less than she does. He is never working. Work he touches goes slightly wrong (crates crooked), and is fine.
3. **He does one visible pointless thing in her field** (sorts, counts, stacks, reads the wrong way up).
4. **He says one thing, once.** Joke first clause, true thing second. If she does not take it, he does not say it again. The true thing is available later.
5. **He leaves while she is looking at the thing**, not at him. The shipped fence "is empty" is the model.
6. **The echo rule (the payoff):** every true thing Lark says is later said *plainly* by someone else who has never heard him (a villager, a Helper, Berit's margin), and later still *done* by the farmer. The player gets the click on the second hearing; the farmer gets it by acting. Lark is never thanked.
7. **One visit per chapter (a scene in which he speaks), plus at most one sorting-trace bark (a thing found in the wrong order; no speech).** The shipped chapter 4 already has two appearances (`c4_no`, `c4_library`; the library is a sorting trace in effect); I add none there. In chapter 6 the shipped Broker and supper are his visits; the sorting trace is the spoons. He is a visit, never a presence. **No new joke without a seed** (§6).
8. **The Helpers are the only evidence of what he is** (slice 03 §5.2): the bunny never thumps at him; the alpaca bows; the tortoise lifts a lid a notch. Nobody comments.

## 1.3 What he never says, never does 💡

Never: *fire, barn, lantern* in a joke before the telling (and he makes **no joke at all** during the telling, slice 04); "you should", "you must", "you're enough", "it wasn't your fault", "rest" or "stop" as an order; Mumyo's name (his one hint is the nonsense name "Mr. Mumble"); a question he already knows the answer to (that is pushing); anything that answers Q9–Q13 for her; anything about Berit that blames; any bell (no bells, ✅). Never solves (a crate is stacked, not carried); never explains a joke; never repeats a missed joke; never pushes. He never touches the melody except to name it once (E-5.M21).

**The one thing that is not a joke, planned:** at the supper he stops sorting the spoons and lets them lie (E-6.M29). It is a gesture, not a grade; it marks that she can now be near something unarranged. (Option B for the parent: one plain word, "Better.". The reviewer's objection is that it grades her humming; I recommend the gesture.)

# 2. Mumyo's traces

## 2.1 The shape of the ladder: a conversation she does not know she is having 💡

Mumyo's want is that somebody looks (rung 3); his wrong belief is that if she saw him she would send him away (rung 4, proved on the night of the fire). So every trace is an **invitation with an easy yes** (leave it, step round it, smile, set out a cup) and every trace is **shaped by how the last one was answered**. This gives the ladder a cause chain the player can feel without being told.

## 2.2 The rule-set (14 rules) 💡

1. **Harmless.** Moved, never lost; nothing broken or taken; nothing in the player's inventory changes (the game never punishes).
2. **He never does her work.** He plays with its leftovers (pulled weeds, rocks, thatch ends) and offers tools in his own size. He cannot be made to work.
3. **Every trace is an invitation with an easy yes.** The text must leave one cheap way to say yes (step round, leave it, put out a cup). It never *demands* a reply.
4. **The farmer does not look.** Her habit, not an order: "you look the other way" (shipped `c3_bell`) is allowed once per chapter at most; elsewhere she simply does not look up.
5. **Escalation by trust, not by size.** Refused, he gets smaller and farther (ch2 after ch1). Accepted, he gets bolder (ch3).
6. **Bell grammar** ✅💡: quick jingle-jangle = a prank is coming; a single *tink* = sorry; two soft rings = "I'm here"; silence = ashamed. **The player needs only two sounds: *tink = sorry* and *two soft rings = here*, each heard twice before chapter 6:** *tink* in ch1 (the hush) and ch3 (the mug); two rings in ch1 (`c1_bell_out`) and ch5 (the second flower morning, E-5.M22). The jingle, the silence and the village's three rings are decoration. Texts say "a very small bell", never "his bells". Bells are the only small bells that ring by themselves (slice 02).
7. **Light only on wet ground and petals, never near anything dry.** He made lights in the barn; since the fire he makes none near hay, wood, thatch or the farmer's hands. His light is the marsh and its flowers (Pieter sees it from the Wall in ch4); the garden is where it is lit on purpose, and the first light that someone *stands beside*. No sparks in any picture: slice 01's "a spark" becomes **a glint, as of a very small bell** (§8).
8. **Dusk and dawn for what is seen; by day only warm stones.** He acts at the edges of the day, the hour of the fire. # 3. The melody

## 3.1 What it is, as a shape (the composer's note; never player text) 🔒 💡

Tune B has **four phrases**: the **Rise**, the **Turn**, the **Wait**, and the **Home**. The Wait ends on a held beat of silence, **a rest**. The Home begins after the rest. The farmer hums Rise, Turn, Wait and, instead of resting, goes straight back to the Rise. She hums it as a **loop**: it never lands. That is "badly hummed". (The Loop is also this book's comic structure; the melody is her spine set to music.)

The **Home** is the one phrase the village already has: it is **Barnaby's lullaby ending** and the grandmothers' *Last Bit* (slice 02, wordless, "nobody knows why it is there"). It has been in the valley's cradles since long before the fire. **Berit lifted it out and put the rhyme on it**: tune A, the kitchen song. So: **A = Home with words.** When Fenna was little she heard A; at fifteen she hears B and half-recognises its end. That is why the tune that "followed her all week" feels like something she knows and does not know.

| Phrase | Who carries it besides the farmer | Where in the story |
|---|---|---|
| Rise | Tom whistles it to the sheep; Hilde hums it at the Stones | ch2–4 |
| Turn | Pieter whistles it, not knowing (shipped `c3_pieter_paid`) | ch3 |
| Wait | Rosa's lost bit (the tune she can never finish needs a rest) | ch3 (02 E-3.L05) |
| Home | Barnaby (the Last Bit), the grandmothers, Berit (A), then everyone at the supper | whole book; ch6 |

Tune A's six lines (the rhyme) each carry the Home notes; the sound plan (§3.5) adds one more note to A's closing figure per Helper (slice 03's six-note hook), so the sixth completes it.

## 3.2 The rules of the melody 💡

1. **It sounds only when someone is still.** The stones brighten while she stands still; she hums best when her hands are empty. (The first-night awe in 01 already has this; I make it a law.)
2. **It does nothing.** No healing, no solving, no gifts (shell rule 1; memory not magic). What it changes is **pace**: people who hum it walk a breath slower. Pieter walks slower down the road; Greenstem stops dead; Rosa sits down. (Not "late": slice 07 has Pieter's lateness as a kindness.)
3. **The animals pause at the rest** the farmer skips: the hens go quiet, the goat stops mid-chew, for exactly the length of the held beat, and then look at each other when she hums on. (The Rest Gag, E-1.M04.)
4. **Who hears it:** the farmer; Minena (and the room); the Helpers (they go still); the squirrel; Lark (he comments once); Mumyo (he goes silent to listen; he is the only one who rings when Home lands); Barnaby, Rosa, Pieter, Tom, Hilde, Ansel, Greenstem in fragments; Quill, late, while counting. **Who does not:** the Lord's people on the Lord's business (Reeve, the Count: never), the magpie (never leaves the shell), Wren (she does not hum: she is the one person in the book who is not looking for it).
5. **It is not Mumyo's song and not Berit's lullaby to Minena** (✅). Mumyo listens; he does not own it.
6. **Hum, don't whistle** (slice 02's rule). Folk logic 💡: whistling calls; humming keeps company. Pieter whistles it in the open; that is why the Lord's man is the first to be slowed by it.
7. **The forest answers the rule:** the Deep will not be hurried (§4.3). The shell is out of reach for anyone who runs.
8. **It is never completed in the farmer's mouth alone.** The only complete performance in the game is the supper (§3.3). Her old-age hum in the frames stops at the rest by choice.
9. **It never has words of its own.** Only A has words, and A is the rhyme about the Helpers. The Home has words only as the Bargain's rhyme.
10. **The alpaca's long note is her own** (03: the alarm) and unrelated to the Wait; do not score them alike.

## 3.6 How the deepest layer shapes the text and stays out of it 🔒🔒

**Never in any text:** the idea that she is complete, enough, whole, worthy, already home; "buddha", "wholeness", "nature". **Banned words in melody scenes** (a checklist for whoever writes): *enough, whole, complete, worth, deserve, perfect, belong, right (as in all right), safe*. (They are fine elsewhere. "Small is plenty" is Berit's and stays.)

**How the form carries it (a writing checklist, each rule invisible to the reader):**
1. **A rest before the last line.** In any box that touches the melody, the last sentence comes after a break (a page break, or a one-line paragraph). The reader's breath is the rest.
2. **Nobody hurries.** No sentence in a melody scene contains a rush word; the farmer's body stops first (hands go still, a bucket is set down). The stop is stated physically, never as a decision.
3. **It never says what the melody wants or means.** Only what the listener does and what the things around her do.
4. **The first time it is named it is a joke** (Lark); after that it is "the tune".
5. **The Home is never described.** The Wait is described by what pauses.
6. **Berit's margin notes never mention the tune.** (She hummed it as a song; she wrote nothing.)
7. **The supper sentence barely changes.** Shipped "You know the tune. You sing it, too." becomes "You know the song. You sing it, too." (the word *tune* belongs to B) plus one clause where her hum runs under the singing (E-6.M29). The scene shows her **joining a thing already sung**, never completing the tune; "Home lands" is a composer's note and never a scene word.

## 3.7 The text-only melody (what survives with no sound and no art) 💡

The stones that answer a still body (A1), the Rest Gag barks, "Yours." and "it only plays when somebody stands still" (E-5.M21), the page break before the last line of every melody box, the supper clause, the frame clause (a finger lifted). Lost without sound: the Wait, the Home, the Line glow. **Test with a child:** does the supper land on its text and pacing alone? If not, the clause gets a page of its own and a one-line paragraph before "You sing it, too."
