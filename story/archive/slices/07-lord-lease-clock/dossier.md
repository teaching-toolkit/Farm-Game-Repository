# 0. The slice in one page

**The idea (💡).** The Lord's pressure is a **staircase the child can see from chapter 1** (slice 01's sacks: one in ten, one in five, one in three, one in two) and climbs herself because the Hurst's Book climbs a rung whenever it **hears the farm is alive**. The top step is **the claim** (the lease says a half). Below runs a **family clock whose asks soften while the dread under them rises**: the letters from home ask less and less (come home, stay if you must, may I visit, a question about a bucket, "I am not asking anything") until the family simply arrives, and the farmer, who misreads each softer ask as a warning, does not believe the last until the Fair. The two clocks cross at chapters 4 to 5 and meet at the Fair.

**The deadline the child can see.** The game already shows a **season countdown** (questions left until the season turns). Every season end is **Share Day** (02's almanac: Pieter walks the valley, stools go out, the Hall serves a Share Day soup "so nobody pays on an empty stomach"). The Lord's thread hangs its occasions on that visible date and adds one *thing to be ready for* each chapter (§2).

**Three load-bearing inventions.**

1. **The Book has three columns and the Lord's has one.** *Taken* (red ink), *carried down* (a grey line at the foot of every page), *given back* (Quill's pencil, then his ink). The burnt page in chapter 1 was burnt along the edge that held another column. Quill's arc is getting the third column into the Book: the **Bargain as arithmetic**.
2. **The Lord is the man who did not open his post.** He reads the last line of every grey letter and puts it in a drawer; the middle lines offered patience three years running. The Count (bible only) is an old man whose number is bread. **It reaches the player** in one line from Quill after the ink.
3. **The Table.** One line at the foot of the old lease: *never so much that the table is bare.* It is the legal ancestor of the engine's quarter-of-the-till cap, of Pieter's secret mercy, of the mother's "the table is long", of the Open Bowl and of the Fair. **The Fair is the answer because it is a table**: the farm does not argue the claim, it lays the thing the Book has no column for, and Quill counts the plates by name.

**Resolution (recommended 💡, unchanged):** the Lord **learns one thing and goes quiet**. He reads the middle lines, signs Quill's count with a *crooked* seal, adds **a hook-outside paper for every barn**, and returns a pie plate: "Too sweet. Send another." *More*, at last harmless. He does not lose, repent aloud, appear or come to the Fair. A tenth stays as long as the farm stands.

## 6.2 What lateness is (replaces the Hold)

While something is owed the child cannot buy with coins (seeds, items, builds); selling, making from stock, farming and trips go on. The game's own message says it: *The Lord's share first: pay Pieter at the gate.* The story says no more than that. Its warm face is **other people being patient**: Rue turns her cart round and waits; Pieter has walked the long way round; a hen lays an egg and you may *sell* it. No seizure, no eviction, no scolding, no barter that does not exist.
