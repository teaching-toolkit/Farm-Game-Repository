## 2. The Rule of Three Dresses 💡 (a joke engine)

"A rumour has three dresses: one to go out in, one to come home in, and one for best." A fact told three times in Hazelwood changes in a predictable, funny way. Use it for barks (almanac barks B14, B15 and B28):

| The deed | Hop 1 (the one who saw it) | Hop 2 (the cart or the Stones) | Hop 3 (back at the Kettle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| the farmer gives the seller one bowl of porridge | "she gave me porridge" | "she's feeding the road" | "she cooked for forty and the ghost did the washing-up" |
| a roof is finished | "a leaning roof" | "a roof that leans" | "the house is tilted and it's on purpose" |
| the pony carries an order | "a pony with an order" | "the pony runs the farm" | "the pony is the new mayor" |
| Pieter is seen near the farm | "Pieter visited" | "Pieter was seen near Moonedge" | "Pieter was on the roof" |
| the badger pours tea | "a badger with a pot" | "a badger with a very good pot" | "the badger is the healer" |

The comedy: the rumour grows by one hop's worth of generosity each time, which means the village's gossip about the farmer is **always more flattering than the truth**. This is how the farm's reputation (the game's `trust` rules) can rise faster than the farmer's own sense of herself, and why her spine (keep useful) cannot catch up with the praise.

## 3. What is not in dispute (the "known" list) 💡

Everyone in Hazelwood agrees on these, and any of them can be said in a piece without a source:

1. The fire was in the late summer, after a long dry spell, at dusk.
2. It began in the barn and took the barn, the house and the sheds.
3. **Nobody was hurt.** The household was woken by the alarm (the village says "the animals made a noise"; the alpaca's note 🔒).
4. The village came with buckets and made a chain from the stream at the foot of the rise up to the barn, about a hundred paces; it was too late for the buildings. Ansel rang the first **five fast, one slow**; Pieter saw the glow from the Wall.
5. The Lord ended the lease. The family left in the winter, by night, across the Alder on Otto's ferry and away by the Far Bank road, in silence.
6. A young girl (Berit's grandchild) and her grandmother went to a cottage in the hills; the rest went to Weir.
7. Moonedge has stood empty since. The woods have crept in. The bells have been heard. "Bell Hill" is what the children call it.
8. The Moonedge Mark was chalked from the first Share Day after the fire.

### The Lightning Ballad as a mercy 💡 → 04 (shrunk in revision 2)

V1 is the village's story, and it is **Maud's, and half-deliberate**. The week after the fire, sitting up with the broken family, **Granny Maud** said "lightning" to the first person who asked, because it was kinder than any other word, and because a lightning fire cannot be charged to a family. It spread the way things do in Hazelwood: the Players and the children got hold of it, and a skipping rhyme grew out of it ("Lightning in the night, the little one ran out"). Maud did not plan a conspiracy and did not tell three women. She half-believes it herself now; she has never said it was untrue; and she has never said it was true to the girl. Hilde's Knot-string has a knot for it she has not untied, because she does not know whose it is. Elder Agnes is **not** in it (revision 2 removes the three-woman plot and Agnes's "nine years" of silence). When Maud says, once, to the farmer: "It was a poor thing to do, and I'd do it again" (E-6.L06), it is one line.

This fits ✅: the village "distrusts the whole family" (the resentment is the Mark, not the fire); "most were sorry" (they came with buckets); and the clue `c2_barn` ("lightning does not start low") undoes the ballad in the farmer's eyes only.

### Who half-knows the truth (V6) 💡 → 04 (shrunk in revision 2)

The ✅ cousin saw the lights from a window and then the girl running out (C7, DECISIONS). The village has a *different* half, and it is small: **Otto** saw a small sooty figure sitting dry-eyed by the Hop at dawn, and **Maud** held her. Neither has said it aloud, to the girl or to each other. The rest of the village knows only the ballad. So `c6_crisis` ("the whole square goes quiet") is a real silence, `c6_bend` costs the farmer what the book says it costs, and the village's answer, the Cup, is a custom meeting a surprise, not a rehearsed absolution. **→04: if you want the village to know nothing, shrink V6 to Otto alone.**

## 7. The tune as a rumour 💡 (the melody's village route; ticket 06 owns what it is)

The farmer hums a tune without noticing (✅). Villagers pick it up in this order:

1. **Rosa** hears it first, over the cider (E-3.L05), and cannot finish her own tune without it.
2. **Hilde** hums it at the Stones and does not know where from.
3. **Tom** whistles it to the sheep and is told to hum it in the forest.
4. **Ansel** answers it on the bell without knowing.
5. **Old Barnaby** has always hummed the last phrase of it (a lullaby ending nobody can place) 🔒.
6. By the Fair, everyone hums it, and the shipped `c6_supper` ("Somebody starts to sing. You know the tune") is the village singing a tune it believes is its own.

The tune does not come from the village, only reaches it by her. What it *is* stays 🔒🔒 and in 06's hands. This file only describes the carrier.

## 9. Cautions

1. **Do not make the village the villain.** Their grievance is a chalk mark on bread; their kindness is a bucket and a lie. Both are real.
2. **The village is not told the truth by a narrator.** It half-knows; it chooses silence.
3. **The player does not get this file as exposition.** It lets pieces *sound* as if the village knows something.
