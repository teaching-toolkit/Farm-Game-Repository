## 1. The book in one sentence

A girl who keeps busy so that nobody will ask what she did comes alone to the ashes of her family's farm to make one seed grow, but the farm can only live again if she stops doing it alone and lets six lost Helpers and a village back in. The player is that farmer (second person, the player's name, no gendered word, no looks, never described); for the architects she is **Fenna**, female, 15 at the start, Minena's grandmother ✅. Frame: a very old you in bed, grandchildren on the floor ("Tell us about the farm that burned down"); seven bedside scenes; the frame never interrupts a chapter ✅.

## 2. The locked ✅ facts (full text: `../RUN-CHARTER.md` §3)

Six chapters, six Helpers (bunny, tortoise, goat, pony, badger, alpaca) who do not talk, a six-line rhyme that comes back one line per return. The fire began in the hay barn while the girl played with Mumyo; the Lord (Wexley) took more each year and owes upward; the chain reaction dried the land; the family split. One stalk, one grain, the bunny kept the patch alive. The melody comes from the conch (deepest layer 🔒🔒, never in any text). The frame's last line: *"If you meet a little fellow with bells, tell him I was never angry."* The steward arrives in chapter 4; no religion; game time is never counted in player texts (D11). The cousin is a girl. Free play after chapter 6, no epilogue chapter. The bend triggers automatically at the Fair. Poison costs all coins (a parent exception). Pictures never show the farmer's face.

## 3. The hidden truth 🔒 and its settled form

- **The barn night.** The lantern hung on its hook *outside* the barn door; the fire began inside, in the hay, while the girl played with Mumyo; the lantern came in during the play, and Fenna does not know whose hands (hers, his lights, both): the one question the book never answers. There is **no Lord's order.** Causes layered: the share (every hand at the far field, the dry well), a moved warning (the alpaca was moved to the far paddock; Berit: "I was busy"), a lonely child playing, a lonely maker of lights, a lantern that came in.
- **Her last words** to Mumyo: "This is all your fault! Go away! I never want to see you again!" and she told the grown-ups it was him (04-27). So she never sees him again ✅ and cannot honestly say "I was never angry"; the request goes through Minena.
- **Liv** saw both: the lights (she took them for her mother, Tilda) and Fenna running out with the empty bucket; she told the Reeve the second and kept the first (`../characters/liv-tilda-berit.md`).
- **The dented bucket:** one childhood bucket (Fenna's own, in Berit's box): hauled at dawn for the tortoise's dry pond and for the field; a pass, praise, and empty at the fire. Bucket Night's buckets are a separate set of objects.
- **The cost the book keeps:** a tenth for as long as the farm stands; the Lord keeps his debt; the village risked the Hurst; "go away" cannot be taken back.

## 4. The Lord, the lease and the clock

**Claim versus collection.** The lease says **a half** (the claim). The collection climbs a ladder of the engine's own steps (a tenth, a fifth, a third, the half), collected by Pieter, counted by Quill from chapter 4; the farm is not on the Roll until Quill's signed count, so paying does not lower the **Moonedge Mark** (a chalk thin moon on every loaf, "until the farm gives again"; a fixed grievance, ended as a village act in chapter 6, before Quill's paper). The quarter-of-the-till cap binds from chapter 5, so the felt rates are about 6, 19, 25, 25 per cent; Pieter takes the lesser (his mercy, his post at risk). One reason for the rise: the Hurst hears the farm is alive. The child has no lever. The first hour already shows the ladder as sacks (a tenth, a fifth, a third, a half): the new farm replays the old (01-8 confirmed). The **Table** ("never so much that the table is bare") is carried three ways: Berit's salt card (ch3), Pieter's mercy scene (ch5), the tin and the lease (ch4). **The Lord** is never seen (07-40): a crooked seal, a second sheet "Hook outside, hay inside. For every barn in the valley. -W.", "Too sweet. Send another. -W."; he did not open his post (he read the last line of the grey letters and never the middle), told in one line by Quill (07-38). **The Count** and Greywater never appear in text (07-39); the player meets only grey barges, ink, wax and a block of grey salt. One in-story occasion per chapter, never a fail state: `ending.md` (the clock table).

## 6. The five silences (what the book never says)

1. Whose hands carried the lantern in. 2. What Berit felt (her guilt is in the alpaca page only). 3. What the father does beyond a hand on a lantern and a folded tent. 4. What the Count is. 5. Whether Mumyo forgives (a sound, a thimble, and the request through Minena).

## 7. Mumyo, Lark, the melody (rules that bind every scene)

**Mumyo** ✅ hand-sized imp, white hair, grey vest of bells, lonely, the girl's only playmate; **no voice**; present only as traces (bells, moved things, a night garden); the player sees nine glimpses, a different part each time, never the face; "the farmer is never looking". His **name reaches the player only in the garden telling** (00-3, flag in `../QUESTIONS.md`); Lark's one hint is "Mr. Mumble" (ch5). **Lark** ✅ practically immortal, rust coat, odd buttons, "Probably fine."; true things as jokes; never pushes, never solves; **one visit and one sorting trace per chapter**; no bells. **The melody:** comes from the conch; four phrases (06 §3); the rhyme is sung to Berit's kitchen song (tune A), the melody (tune B) is kept for the frames (03-15); the old you hums it in the frames (01-7); Minena may hum the true last phrase (queued). **Awe:** at most 8 in the game, at most 2 per chapter, never adjacent; the master plan uses five (`master/ending.md`).
