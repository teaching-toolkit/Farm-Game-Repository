## 1. What is decided ✅

> **Address rule (8 Oct 2026, overrides every mention of "Fenna" or "she" below for player-facing text):** the player *is* the farmer
> and makes their own avatar, boy or girl. In-game texts speak to **you**; people call you by **the name the player chose**; the frame is
> **grandchildren asking you** for a story (no Grandma/Grandpa, no Oma/Opa). "Fenna" (female) is only our working name. The farmer's
> character stays at the current level of detail and shows mostly through how the world reacts to you (`characters/fenna.md`).

- **The farmer is the young Grandmother, a teenager of about 15. Her name is Fenna.** The player renames her; "Fenna" is the default
  and what the architects use. **Minena is not the farmer.** Minena is the child in the bedside frame.
- **The story is a memory.** Fenna tells it to Minena. The frame (Minena at the bedside) appears **once at the very beginning and
  once between chapters**. *Inside* a chapter there is only the story itself, popping up between achievements, goals and problems
  the player meets.
- **The bulk of the story is what happens in the present of the game.** On top of that runs a **mystery** uncovered slowly across
  the whole game: *why did the farm burn, and what is up with the magical pets?*
- **The fire started in the hay barn while the girl was playing with Mumyo.** The imp caused it. As a child she saw an imp nobody
  believed, and later was never sure whether she saw a real imp or caused the fire herself and pushed the thought away. Part of her
  family suspected who caused it, and she had a hard time afterwards.
- **Why the farm was in trouble:** the family worked the land for a **local Lord** who always wanted more. They extracted more and
  more and dried the land out through a chain reaction they did not understand or see coming. The Lord made even young children
  work hard from early morning till late evening. **The Lord himself owes someone above him**, so he is not a cartoon villain.
- **The imp as longing:** Mumyo appeared as a manifestation of the child's suppressed need for mischief and for being a child. The
  shell in the forest made her dream of him; sometimes by day she thought she saw him. **After the fire she never saw him again.**
  💡 So in the game Mumyo is present only as **traces** (bells, harmless pranks, moved things, the night garden). Fenna never sees
  him; **the player sometimes does** (a glimpse in a story picture that Fenna does not notice). This keeps the parent's rule, lets
  Mumyo "show up every now and then", and explains why, as an old woman, she asks Minena to give him her message.
- **The family moved away from Hazelwood** after the fire. Fenna has only a **vague acquaintance with the village** from long
  ago. **Reconnecting with the village is something she has to do throughout the story, and the dishes she makes are part of that.**
- **The arrival:** the farm burned **seven years** before (she was 8, now 15). As a teenager she wants to get away from her family
  for a while and goes back to the ruins "to gain perspective". She finds **one seed and one patch of workable ground** at the farm: ✅ a single ripe wheat stalk with only one grain left on it, the rest nibbled off.
  That is where the game begins. The melody draws her to the forest edge; she cannot say why she wants to camp there. Her
  grandmother's death, and the box she leaves, is what makes her take the break.
- **Game time is never counted** (D11). The game covers many seasons (about twelve game years if counted); no text gives a year or
  her age after the opening. Nobody will be counting.
- **The pets** helped the original farmer run the farm. They return one by one, and each gives a **real function** (automation,
  simple loops and if-then tasks, in the spirit of Factorio), not just a perk.
- **A six-line farm rhyme** runs through the story, one line per helper.
- **A steward from the Lord arrives in chapter 4.**
- **The clues are tied to the ruins in the game.**
- **Six chapters, six magical pets:** a medicine chapter goes between the current chapters 4 and 5; its pet is the **badger**, who
  comes back once the player makes really good medicine. The Master Farm stays last.
- **The shell:** the Moonstone Forest houses the mysterious shell. The magical characters appear **inside** it. The squirrel may
  appear in the normal world; the magpie may not.
- **Lark:** a magical, mythical character modelled on Wit from the Stormlight Archive; practically immortal; that never becomes an
  issue.
- **The ending of the frame:** Fenna's final request to Minena: *"If you meet a little fellow with bells, tell him I was never angry."*
- **Minena is Fenna's grandchild, one of many.** Her parents live in another part of the village, but she visits Fenna often and
  **loves the farm**. So the farm lives on after Fenna's story: Fenna settles in Hazelwood, has a big family, and the farm is part of
  the family's life when Minena is a child.
- **The bend (D6):** small bends earlier (the goat in chapter 3, asking the badger in chapter 5), then in chapter 6 she says out
  loud what she saw. "Sounds ok for now": provisional, may change when the outline is written.
- **The suspicious relative:** "maybe an older cousin", flexible.
- **The melody comes from the conch shell** (the shell in the Moonstone Forest; the same conch Minena finds). The deepest layer, the
  "backstory of the backstory" 🔒🔒: the melody is the person's own **buddha nature**, their innate wholeness, projected into the
  world and sounding from the conch. It never appears in any text; it only shapes it. (This is why both Fenna and Minena hear it,
  fifty years apart, when each needs to hear that she is enough.)
- **Who runs the farm in Minena's time:** one of **Minena's uncles or aunts** (Fenna's child).
- **Hazelwood family names stay the same** across the generations (Appleby, Odinson, Solemate, Greenstem …) for continuity with
  *The Forest Within*.
- **No religion.** A pagan-flavoured world: people believe in spirits, fairies and dwarves, lightly, without fervour. No church or chapel.
- **The Helpers do not talk;** you understand them from sounds, nods and looks. **Lord Wexley** is the Lord.
- **Hazelwood and the Moonstone Forest** are the setting. The 28 favour-givers get re-cast to fit (Rounds 3 and 6).

- ❓ **The suspicious relative:** leaning to **an older cousin** (flexible). A cousin who was twelve that summer and saw her near
  the barn is a believable witness and lived in the same household.

## 3. The hidden truth 🔒 (💡 proposal, revised)

**Older than anyone remembers.** The farm stands at the edge of the Moonstone Forest, where the shell lets six **Helpers** step into the
ordinary world. A bargain old as the farm: the Helpers help; the people take only what the land can give back. The Helpers keep
the balance: weeds and clover, water, scrub and compost, the roads, medicine, and the guarding of the flock.

**The Lord and the lease.** The farm is leased from the Lord of Hazelwood. Each year he asks for a bit more, because he owes more
to someone above him. The family, afraid of losing the farm, says yes.

**The chain reaction** (the ecology the game teaches; each link is a game system):
1. No fallow, no rotation → the soil goes thin and pale.
2. Less compost, because every straw is sold → even thinner.
3. Fields cut into the forest edge → the hillside loses its shade and its roots.
4. The spring pond is drained for irrigation → the stream silts and the well drops (and the old mill's wheel stops).
5. The Helpers are worked like tools or told to stay out of the way → they leave, one by one, over three years. Each leaving is a warning nobody reads.
6. A dry summer; the hay is tinder.

**The fire.** Late summer, Y0. The girl (8) has slipped away to the hay barn at dusk to play with Mumyo, the only play she
gets. He makes dancing lights. A lantern tips into the hay. She runs out shouting. House, barn and sheds burn; nobody is hurt.
Mumyo, ashamed, runs back into the shell and does not come out. The last Helper leaves that same night.

**After.** The Lord blames the family, the lease ends, and 💡 he makes sure no one in his lands will take them on. The family has
to leave the district. ✅ **The family is split:** her parents, younger siblings and the older cousin go to a river town several days away (💡 *Weir*); **Fenna and her grandmother Berit** (💡 name) go to a small hill cottage, where Berit raises her from memory, with the book. Fenna was eight; she remembers faces and a few names
from Hazelwood, not much more. A relative (💡 an older cousin, flexible) says she was seen near the barn. She says she saw an imp; nobody believes a
child. In time even she is not sure. Seven years later her grandmother, the farm's old matriarch, dies and leaves her a box: the farm
book in the matriarch's handwriting, a tin pot and a dented bucket. 💡 After the funeral Fenna is meant to move to her parents in
the town, where she has never fitted in. She asks for "a few weeks" first. The same week a melody starts in her head.
**At the farm she finds the one seed** ✅: a single ripe wheat stalk on the one patch that is still good, every grain nibbled off but one. ✅ A mystery to the player at first; it becomes clear when the bunny comes back. *Why is that one patch
still alive?* is the very first small mystery (answered when the bunny comes home: she had been keeping it, all those years).

**The melody** ✅ comes from **the conch shell**. 🔒🔒 Deepest layer (never in any text): it is the listener's own wholeness,
projected into the world and sounding from the shell. It calls the people who most need to hear that they are enough. Fenna hears it
at 15, Minena at 7. Both are busy proving they are good enough: Fenna by never stopping, Minena by being the helpful one.
Lark names the melody in chapter 5 or 6, and does it as a joke. (Clue in the farm book: *Small is plenty.*)

**Mumyo** 🔒 has stayed near the farm all these years, hiding, ashamed, lonely. He tends a garden of night-blooming flowers: an
apology he has never said. 💡 It is in the **River Meadow** (chapter 6), where he and Fenna used to play. He never shows himself to
her (✅ she never saw him again), but he cannot stop leaving traces: pranks that are really invitations to play.

**Lark** 🔒 knows all of this. He tells the truth only as jokes, so no one can be cross, and never pushes.

## 4. The clue ladder 💡

Clues sit in **ruins** and **land clearings**, because clearing one is already a moment where the game stops and gives something back.

**The ruins that exist now** (from `farm-progression.json`): chapter 1 the old workshop · chapter 2 the burnt woodshed, the burnt
barn, the old compost corner · chapter 3 the old mill · chapter 4 the old glasshouse. **Chapter 5 (medicine) and chapter 6 have none
yet**, and the old chapter 5 had only land clearings (orchard terraces, the River Meadow marsh). So:

- **Chapter 5, new ruin** 💡: the matriarch's **still-room** (her herb house). Fits medicine and the badger.
- **Chapter 6:** the **River Meadow marsh** works the same way as a ruin (a clearing with a find), so no extra node is needed.

## 5. The village strand: reconnecting 💡

Fenna left at 8. The village remembers "the Moonedge family" (💡 farm name) and the fire. Some people were kind; some blamed
them; most just remember a thin girl who was always carrying something. **She makes herself useful rather than talking**, which is
her spine, so food is how she speaks.

This maps straight onto what the game already does: **dishes delivered to the village open the merchants**, favours are
requests from neighbours, the notice board carries small jobs, and the last chapter ends at the Harvest Fair.

| Chapter | The village | What a dish does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strangers. A travelling merchant and a seed seller come first. The village sniffs the smoke from the ruin. | Porridge for the merchant; vegetable soup: "the village notices you" |
| 2 | One person remembers her: **young Greenstem**, a boy her age at his family's herb stall 💡. First favours. The quarry folk, the miners, the stall. | Stone soup, bread, pancakes, stew |
| 3 | Someone who was kind to her family (Granny Maud, Berit's old friend 💡); someone who still blames them (the miller, whose wheel nearly stopped when the stream silted 💡; he thaws when he sees the stream run again). The harvest dance. | Cakes, cider |
| 4 | The steward arrives. Does the village stand with her or with the Lord? | Pizza night |
| 5 | Illness in the village: they need her medicine. She is needed, not just tolerated. | Medicine (a new kind of "dish") |
| 6 | The Harvest Fair. Hazelwood is her home again. | Pies, cheese, preserves, cloth |

Notes for the game: the 28 favour-givers become **half-remembered faces**; the notice board becomes a place she is shy of at
first. The friends' postcards (gifts) may come from a family that moved too: someone she has not heard from in seven years.

## 6. The frame: Minena at the bedside 💡

- **Intro (before the game's first goal):** the grandchildren, Minena in front, crowd round your bed (💡 in the farmhouse the player builds
  in chapter 4) and ask: "Tell us about the farm that burned down." You are not sure you remember it right. That is the promise of the mystery.
- **Between chapters:** a short scene each time. Minena asks a question; you answer in one or two lines and sometimes does not
  (the gap *is* the mystery). 💡 Now and then Minena hears tiny bells outside the window; you, a little deaf by now, do not.
- **After the last chapter:** the truth, in your words, and the request ✅: *"If you meet a little fellow with bells, tell him I
  was never angry."* This hands Minena into her own story (*Minena 01*, the conch).
- Seven scenes (intro, between the six chapters, the end), about 120–200 words each. The frame never interrupts play and has nothing
  to do with the quiz, Rest or the economy.
