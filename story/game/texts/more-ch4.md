Compressed from the novella on 14 Nov 2026 (`story/08`). German is in `godot-prototype/data/i18n/data-de.json`.

## c4_hens · scene · Counting hens
*When:* `after:c4_count:2`

Page 1. Quill comes back to count the hens properly. Granny Maud stops dead in the lane. “You can't count hens out loud,” she says. “It's bad luck. One goes missing.” Quill finishes the count in a whisper.

Page 2. Another day he says, “Seven.” Your goat stamps one hoof. Eight times. Quill counts again, jaw set. There is a hen behind the rain barrel. He writes “8”, reaches behind his ear for his pencil, and the pencil is gone. The goat is chewing.

## c4_hoofprints · scene · Hoofprints
*When:* `done:barn_3`

Page 1. The new road has cobbles now, all the way to the village. In the soft earth beside it are two hoofprints, side by side, neat as a pair of shoes. They are at every spot with a good view.

Page 2. You leave a new blanket folded on the fence to air. In the morning it is draped there in artful folds, one corner tucked in, as if for a painting. Next to it, in the mud, two hoofprints.

## c4_landing · scene · Here!
*When:* `after:c4_hens:3`

Page 1. On Landing Day you go down to the river for your letters, like everyone. You asked Mei not to make a fuss, so she just calls the names. “Appleby!” “Here!” “Hewitt!” “Here!” “Moonedge!”

Page 2. Old Jan opens his mouth to shout it, the way he always has. Then he shuts it and looks at you. “Here!” It comes out small. But it comes out. Old Jan beams like a lamp, and the Landing claps, just a little, politely.

## c4_grafts · visit · Sticks with labels
*When:* `after:c4_landing:3`

Page 1. A round brown man comes up the road with a bundle of sticks, looking over his shoulder four times. He is Thorne, the Lord's gardener. “Apple grafts,” he says. “From the old Moonedge orchard. I kept them alive, behind the lemons. They were always yours.”

Page 2. Twenty-two sticks, each with a little label. Moonedge Russet. Moonedge Sweet. Berit's Red. At the bottom of the track stands Quill. Thorne goes the colour of a beetroot. Quill writes something down and walks on. The orchard gets its old apples back.

## c4_thump · scene · The thump
*When:* `after:c4_grafts:3`

Page 1. You want one more plank in the coop door before dark. Under the fence, the bunny thumps. Quick, three at once. “In a minute,” you say, with a nail in your mouth.

Page 2. In the morning there are speckled feathers by the coop. One hen fewer. The bunny sits by the empty nest box and does not thump. She only looks. You bolt the door. The goat sleeps on the coop roof, standing up, every night after.

## c4_share_day · scene · Share Day
*When:* `seen:c4_lease + lord_shares>=4`

Page 1. Quill reads the lease out at your fence, before the village. “… a half of all that the land gives.” Then the new things the Book has found: “One glass kiln. One press. The orchard.” Each lands like a small stone in a bucket.

Page 2. You go inside and come out with the rusty tin. You set it on the rail and open it. Quill stops reading. “This one,” he says slowly, “is longer.” He reads it right down to the bottom, where nobody reads.

Page 3. There is a line there. Quill sits down on a nail keg, without being asked. “Might I copy one line?” He writes it in his ledger: “never so much that the table is bare.” Then he walks down the hill without counting anything at all.

## c4_reeds · scene · The reeds
*When:* `after:c4_thump:3`

Page 1. The goat wants the pond reeds. The tortoise is sitting on them. The goat leans on the tortoise. She butts it, very gently. She lies down beside it and makes a long, sad, whistling face. The tortoise does not move.

Page 2. By evening the goat is asleep next to the tortoise, and the tortoise has moved four inches. The reeds are free. The goat is too embarrassed to eat them, so she eats the daisy on the tortoise's back instead. It grows back by morning.

## c4_bramble · moment · The last bramble
*When:* `after:c4_no:3`

In the cleared land, one bramble is left, as big as a kitchen table. You reach for the shears. The goat steps into the way, chewing nothing, her bent horn towards you. You put the shears down. Something in the bramble rustles. Nobody looks.

## c4_flask · visit · Cold tea
*When:* `lord_shares>=5`

Page 1. “It's a long road,” says Pieter. He has come up it for Share Day, and he sits on your fence without being asked, his lantern still lit in broad daylight. He takes a little flask from his coat and offers it. Cold tea, very sweet.

Page 2. You each take a sip. Then he stands up, straightens his coat, and becomes a watchman again. “Same time next season,” he says.

The third scarecrow has a flower. Just one. Where it came from, no one will say. It is not a scarecrow flower. It is a real one, a little yellow, put there on purpose, by someone who thought the scarecrow looked lonely.

## c4_jar · letter · The empty jar
*When:* `after:c4_mei:3`

Page 1. The pickle jar comes back from the long voyage, empty and clean. Inside are a pressed leaf, a brass button, and a folded paper in a small, round hand you do not know. “Have you still got the bucket? —L.”

Page 2. You look at the bucket. It is in your hand. It is always in your hand: to the well, the barn, the field and back. You did not even know. You hold it a little tighter.

Page 3. Later you hang the old iron hook by the new door, and the bucket on a nail beneath it. You do not look at how well they fit together.

## c4_puddle · scene · Gordon
*When:* `done:road_5`

Page 1. The paved road has reached the Hurst Gate. On the Lord's Mile, the smooth gravel road that was always the best in the valley, there is a puddle. The whole village has come to look. The children have named it Gordon.

Page 2. Mayor Juno wants it filled at once, but nobody dares take gravel from the Hurst's heap. Then somebody notices that the heap is gone. There is only a flat patch of ground. The village laughs. Then everybody goes quiet, all at once, together.

## c4_longnight · scene · My feet hurt
*When:* `season:winter + after:c4_share_day:2`

Page 1. On Longnight you go straight to the soup pot for the ladle. Greenstem is already holding it. “I'll do it.” “I can—” “I know you can. I'll do it.” So you sit down with empty hands.

Page 2. People stand, one by one, and say their one true thing. Then you stand up, without meaning to. You say one small, true thing: “My feet hurt.” The person beside you passes you a cup. It is only cider. It costs more than soup.

## c4_watch · scene · The Watch Walk
*When:* `done:sq_night_watch` · *Register:* T→A · *Move:* Plant

Page 1. Pieter asks you up on the Watch Walk, the town wall where he walks every night. “Want to show you something,” he says. You wanted to be home before dark. But you go.

Page 2. From the top you can see the whole valley, dark and silver. And up on its hill, at the edge of the black forest, one small yellow square of light. A window. It is your window. You have never seen your farm from outside before.

Page 3. Pieter holds your new lantern out over the marsh. “There's a light out there, too. Some nights. It moves like it's dancing.” You see only reeds. “I'm paid to notice, not to say.” He says it anyway, and walks off whistling.
