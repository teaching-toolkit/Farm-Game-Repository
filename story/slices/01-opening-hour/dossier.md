# 1. Why the child got lost (a reading of the seam, not a guess)

The player's first five minutes in the shipped game: the name prompt ("Who is playing?"), a toast, `c1_frame0` (3 pages), `c1_stalk` (3 pages), a hint box, the first card, `c1_book`. Read as a child would, there are six breaks. ❓ The manager should ask what the test child actually did or said (where they stopped, what they asked); this reading is mine, and behaviour beats verdict.

1. **"You" changes owner with no sign.** Both layers are second person present: "You close your eyes." (an old person in bed) and "You pick up the last grain." (a fifteen-year-old in ash). The child has just typed their own name.
2. **No time or place word crosses the seam.** Shipped texts never say *Moonedge*, *long ago*, *fifteen*, or that the grey place is the farm the grandchildren asked about.
3. **The grandchildren are never called anything.**
4. **The first picture of the farm has no caption and (page 1 of the stalk) no picture at all**: `c1_stalk` has pictures for pages 2 and 3 only.
5. **The box is a hint, not a scene.** The first *story* mention of the box's owner comes four chapters later.
6. **Two different tunes are both "an old tune"** (`c1_book` and `c1_night`).

The answer is **redundancy across layers**, **identity by object** (2.2), and a rule of **one new fact per box and at most one new name per box**, with facts spread over the first nine pages, not packed into the frame (3.1).

## 2.2 The three-objects trick 💡 (keep exactly)

The box holds a book, a tin pot and a dented bucket (timeline `world/00-timeline.md`). **The same three things sit in the bedroom, fifty-odd years on.** Frame page 1: Minena brings the tea in a small old tin pot with a bent handle (and spills half); a thin brown book lies on the blanket; a dented bucket by the bed holds her stones and feathers (✅ she collects them). Ten seconds later the player opens a box and recognises them. It pays twice later with no word said: the bucket that hauled water now carries a child's treasures (the bend of the whole book, in one prop), and in every later frame the objects sit in the same places with small changes.

## 2.3 The hum, and the two tunes (split cleanly) 💡

The hum is the thread (✅ she hums the melody without noticing). **Two tunes, never confused:**

| | **Tune A: Berit's kitchen song** | **Tune B: the melody** |
|---|---|---|
| What | the rhyme's tune, plain, with words | wordless; comes from the conch in the forest |
| Who has it | Berit sang it; the rhyme lines come back **one per Helper** | the farmer hums it; the stones answer it |
| First met | `c1_book` ("Berit's kitchen song comes back") | frame0 p3 (the old you hums), `c1_night` |
| In text | called "the song" or "Berit's song", **never "a tune"** | called "the tune" only; never explained |
| Verbs | you *say it over*, you *wait for the next line* | you *hum* it, you *find you are humming* |

**Why you came: one story, canon wins** (`00-premise.md`, `book-file.md:56`: a melody starts in her head the same week and draws her): **Berit gave the means and the permission** (the box, "you went"); **the tune gave the pull** (it has been in your head all week and you cannot say why you wanted to camp at the forest edge). So `c1_night` changes from "a tune you do not know" to "the tune that has followed you all week is coming from those stones" (section 8). That makes the awe an answer to a question, not just a view. ❓ for ticket 06: does the old you hum tune B in every frame (my reading)? It would make Minena's dream in *Minena 01* a recognition, not a discovery.

## 2.4 The grammar fix

The farm layer keeps second person present (✅). To show that "you" has moved, the first farm line starts with a place and a season ("Moonedge. Early spring."). Frames keep "Back in the room" or a room smell.

## 2.5 How the frame is felt again later, without interrupting a chapter

Chapter 1 is an hour long and the seam is not re-anchored until `c1_frame1`. Two ways, both inside the charter rule:

**Option A: the echo kit (free; recommended now).** (1) The three objects, with repeated small details (the bent handle, a dent that fits a thumb). (2) The hum in three barks, never explained. (3) The old you never speaks inside a chapter; *Berit* does, by note and margin, a second voice from the past. (4) The scroll keeps frames in a "Bedtime" group. (5) The rest screen's *picture* (not text) alternates a young figure lying straight on straw and a lamp-lit bed. ✅ no story piece shows during Rest, so this is chrome; ❓ test with a child that the old person's bed on the rest screen does not confuse (F8).

**Option B: "Where were we?"** (kept; a UI ticket, not outline work): a player-opened button in the scroll plays a two-page recap spoken by the grandchildren, one per chapter (about 40 words each). The player chooses it, so nothing interrupts. Needs a decision from the parent eventually (queued, not blocking).

*(Option C, a one-line frame bark, is dropped: the charter rule forbids it.)*

## 7.1 Fenna (the farmer at fifteen) 💡 unless marked

**Rung 1: what she wants.** To make the one grain grow, by herself, and have something to show before the town takes her. She will not say "I am afraid of the table." She says: "A while."
**Rung 2: the embarrassed want.** Someone to say that it is allowed to stop. And to play.
**Rung 3: the drive.** Keep busy, keep useful, never stop ✅. **The slightly wrong belief** ✅: if I am useful every minute, nobody can be cross with me, and nobody will ask what I did.

**What she remembers of Moonedge at eight**: the kitchen window facing the forest; the well chain; the worn notch she stood on a crate to reach (E-1.19); the smell of hay; her own bucket, too big. **What she does not know**: why the Helpers went; what the numbers meant.

**Behaviour the world can react to** (others tease it; the text never explains it): eats standing up (Rue copies her); carries the bucket everywhere (a hen moves in; Tom asks if she takes it to bed); hums and stops; sets a bucket by every fire; keeps her hat low in the village and her back to the manor road; never sits on the stool. **Quoted lines for the farmer: two only** ("A while." and "I'm fine."), never inside a joke; the frame is the place for a quoted line (`fenna.md`: the spine shows through the world, not through "you").
