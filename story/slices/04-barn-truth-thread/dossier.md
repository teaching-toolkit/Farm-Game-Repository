# 1. The thread in one page

**What it is.** The fire in the hay barn as a mystery the player pieces together over six chapters and is finally *told*. The truth (✅ facts) is fixed in `night-account.md`. This slice decides *how many hands* were on the fire and *who could not say theirs*.

**Five pieces, five silences.** Everyone at Moonedge held one piece and had a reason not to say it: the father lit the lantern and left the children, then said "Not a word"; the mother let him; Berit moved the alpaca ("I was busy"); Liv watched the lights and told only the safe half; Fenna does not know whose hands carried the lantern in, and shouted at the one who might have. **The bend is the first time anyone says theirs, and the player's farmer goes first.** Not everyone mends on the page.

**The true thing goes in the margin.** Berit wrote in margins; Tilda answered there; the mother writes her real sentences in brackets; the father speaks through a horse; Liv leaves stones; the village sings the truth as a ballad about the weather. The telling moves the true thing from the margin into the text. *Never said in the book.* **Looking and telling** (`liv-family-berit.md` §7) is the second shape.

**The bucket is the one object used twice**: a pass ("nobody asks a child with a bucket"), then empty, then the dent that fits the empty hook, then "Have you still got the bucket?", then set down at the Fair, then a seat in the garden, then holding Liv's stones.

**The spine at work.** Keep busy so nobody asks what you did. The thread's jokes are the spine's (the woodpile, buckets at every corner, the guest bed, hiding the bucket) and so is its cost: the telling is the one thing she does that is not work.

**Register.** Mostly **T** and **W**; comedy from the other slices must sit beside it (§12). The thread's own G is spine-driven.

# 5. What the village knows (slice 02, revision 2; full table `night-account.md` §8)

Hazelwood **knows** dusk, a dry month, the barn first, nobody hurt, the family leaving by night. **Only Otto and Maud** know more: a small sooty girl sat dry-eyed by the Hop at dawn; Maud held her and heard "the lights". It **believes** an accident of a dry summer and **says** the Ballad, which began as Maud's one kind word. It does not know about Mumyo or the lantern. So the silence after `c6_crisis` is real and the telling is news; what receives it is the **Cup**. The farmer thinks the Mark and the sideways looks are about her; they are about the Lord.
