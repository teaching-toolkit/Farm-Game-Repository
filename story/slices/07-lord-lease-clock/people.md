## Lord Wexley ✅ name · 💡 the rest

| Rung | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 Want | that the number stops being bigger than he is |
| 2 Embarrassed want | to be told what the number is *for*, by someone who isn't afraid of it |
| 3 Spine ✅ | **More** (a man who answers every fear with a bigger demand) |
| 4 Wrong belief | **if I never open the bad thing, it cannot be as bad as it is** |
| 5 Origin 🔒 | inherited the debts at twenty; his first grey letter had a hard last line; he never read the rest again |

- **Afraid, not wicked** ✅. A man with a drawer. He never appears. Everything he is shows as objects: a red wax seal, a coin face "that has had a long day", the Hurst chimney, the Lord's Mile and its puddle, a gong rung once, a drawer.
- **What he learns** (chapter 6, offstage): he reads the middle lines of every grey letter in one night and sees Quill's page drawn as sacks. He signs the count with a **crooked** seal. **The player is told, once, by Quill, after the ink:** *"He had them all in a drawer. The last line he read. Never the middle."*
- **His resolution is an act, not a speech:** a second sheet with the paper, in his own hand: **"Hook outside, hay inside. For every barn in the valley. —W."** (Berit's rule, the village's rite, adopted as law.)
- **What he does not do:** lose, repent aloud, appear, come to the Fair.
- **His only line, ever** (free play, E-6.R31): on a returned pie plate, *"Too sweet. Send another. —W."* *More*, at last harmless. 💡 (the reviewer: keep.)

## Master Quill ✅ arrives chapter 4 · 💡 arc

| Rung | Answer |

| 3 Spine | **Get the numbers right**, and let nobody argue with a number that is right |
| 4 Wrong belief | **if the number is right, someone above must listen**. (Sibling of the farmer's belief and the Lord's.) |
| 5 Origin 🔒 | a boy clerk, trained in the three-column form at Greywater, lent to the Hurst. He **copied the half into the roll** with his own initial in the corner (the "Q." on the lease). The same year he wrote a true report that the Lord did not open (the farm would be dead in five years). It took four. |

**What he is really after.** (1) The Lord wishes to know why the old farm smokes: *an assessment*. (2) He volunteered, to see whether he was wrong: **a counter-example**, one farm taxed lightly that gives back more than it takes, in numbers a clerk downriver cannot refuse. (3) To be **read**, and to undo a line he wrote. The shipped "gives back more than it takes", said "as if it were a question" (`c4_quill_out`), is his hypothesis said aloud for the first time; "by mistake" (`c4_no`) is his experiment being allowed to begin.

**He is the pressure in chapter 4, and the child feels it** (revision 2): in the reading (E-4.R42) he reads aloud the lines the Book has found (the **glass kiln**, the **cheese vat**, the orchard) and the Pay popup's number rises that Share Day. *He found the glass kiln; now they'll tax the glass kiln.* His honesty raises the bill. (Only things `assumed()` really counts are named; never the farmhouse.)

## Pieter, the night watchman ✅ (share from chapter 3)

What the Lord's clock needs from him (his friendships, stool and Wall are 02/05):

- **He is the rung's messenger.** Every chapter's first Share Day he says one plain line about the new step, with an apology (§ per-rung lines in the dossier): the Hurst has *heard*.
- **His lantern has a public reason and a private cause** (both stand). Public (02): lit by day so people see him coming and have time to put the kettle on or hide. Private (04, never said here): a dark that glows, his minute on the Wall. The Lord's thread uses only the public reason.
- **His secret mercy belongs to chapter 5** (revision 2): where the cap binds he writes **the lesser of the book and a quarter of the till**, and asks the farmer not to tell the Hurst, because the lease has one line at the bottom ("never so much that the table is bare") that nobody reads. In chapter 3 the book's figure *is* what he takes; he claims no discount. A debtor who insists on paying *more* and a collector who refuses is a Mirror Crack for both. **Cost to him:** the difference goes in the grey line; his post rides on it; the Fair rescues him.
- **His lantern, held** (E-5.R19): in the dim lean-to it finally lights a throat. After the tenth (05, free play) it stays unlit.
- *Voice:* "I only carry the book." · "Most people hide when they see me." · "It's the same as last time, only more." · "Nobody reads the bottom of the page."
