## A. Seven real choices

1. **Have you read the novella (`novella/story-retold.md`)?** The plan was that you read it before it becomes pieces. 💡 If not:
   let the pieces be built from it anyway, chapter by chapter, and read each chapter's pieces in play instead. Texts are easy to change.
2. **Why does the whole village say the fire was "lightning"?** 💡 Maud's kind word: that night Granny Maud heard a child shout
   "the lights!" and said "lightning" on purpose, so nobody would blame the child; the travelling players made it a ballad. (Other
   options: a word nobody owns; the Lord's word, which breaks canon.)
3. **The name Mumyo and your rule "no name before frame 6".** The telling is in two halves: at the Fair in public, and at night in
   the garden, where the name stands alone on a page, just before frame 6. 💡 Keep the two halves, and keep your rule literally: the
   garden page says "you", the name first appears in frame 6.
4. **"Where was everyone?"** The hidden cause (every hand was at the far field because of the Lord's share, so the child was alone
   in the barn) is in no passage. A line at the Fair would say it: "Where was everyone?" "The far field. Everyone was always at the
   far field." 💡 Yes: it is the one line that ties the share to the fire, and it stays a child's answer, not an explanation.
5. **The Count.** He is never in any text, so a reader cannot even ask who he is. One child at the grey barge could ask "Who's on
   the barge?" and be hushed. 💡 Yes: the question becomes askable and stays unanswered.
6. **Lark after the Fair.** The novella has him stay for breakfast. 💡 No breakfast (the bible says "a visit, never a presence");
   keep the visit and the white moonstone button.
7. **The mill: sails or a water wheel?** 💡 Sails: the game's mill is a windmill, its picture exists, and two pieces already say sails.
