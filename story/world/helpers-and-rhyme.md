## The farm rhyme ✅ idea, 💡 text

Older than the book. Berit sang it to Fenna as a small child and **never wrote it down** (💡). Fenna half-remembers it: in chapter 1
she has only the first line, and **each further line comes back to her when its Helper comes home**. Memory, not magic, which suits a
girl who is not sure what she remembers. The book's first page, in Berit's hand, says *Start small. Small is plenty.* Six lines, in
the order the Helpers come home.

> Long ears sow the clover green,
> Slow shell keeps the water clean.
> Brave goat clears the bramble bed,
> Strong pony brings the world its bread.
> Wise badger heals what aches and stings,
> Soft alpaca guards all things.

Notes: simple words, one picture per line; "brings the world its bread" means carries and trades; "all things" stays big on purpose. The German
version is made with the rhyme intact in Round 7. ❓ Does the rhyme need a seventh line that is spoken at the end (💡 "Take what is given, give something back")?

## The matriarch, Berit 💡

Fenna's grandmother (her father's mother). Born Y−62; dies Y+7 at 69. Wrote the book. As a child saw the Helpers; as an old woman was
overruled and watched them go. She never forgave herself, and never said so. She raised Fenna in the hill cottage from memory, and her
last gift is the box. (Flagged invention: name, ages.)
